## What the rule checks

Two measures (#182 item 1, owner ruling 2026-11-12); the detail opens with the one that judged, "judged on gpsd TOFF" or "judged on read time, no TOFF".

**TOFF, the primary measure.** sloth's one `?WATCH` line asks for `"pps":true`, under which gpsd emits "the TOFF JSON message on each cycle and a PPS JSON message when the device issues 1PPS" [5]. TOFF carries `real_sec`/`real_nsec`, the receiver's time "for the start of the cycle", and `clock_sec`/`clock_nsec`, the host's clock "when the first timing message of the cycle was received" [5]: both stamped by gpsd, so `real - clock` carries none of sloth's read latency. `toff_judge()` (src/alerts/rules_gnss.c:652) walks the TOFF ring (`gnss_hist_t.toffs`, src/gps.c `take_toff()`) and, from the TOFF anchor, takes the step as the change of `real - clock` plus the host's wall-against-monotonic step between sloth's two reads (read together, so also latency-free): the receiver against the monotonic clock, as below. It fires when that step is at least the threshold and holds, within half of it, on the next TOFF; the leap-second, midnight and rollover shapes (`step_benign()`) and the suspend test apply as below. Consecutive TOFFs are compared, never a TOFF with a fix: gpsd does not send one TOFF per TPV (7 TOFF to 14 TPV in 5 s at the second site, 2026-11-12, and no PPS). Two TOFFs more than 5 s apart on the monotonic clock, the first TOFF, and the first after a no-fix TPV or a lost source are re-anchored on, not judged. A positioned fix with a TOFF read within 3 s of it (`GNSS_TOFF_FRESH_MS`) is TOFF's to judge: the read-time measure only takes it as its anchor, so it never fires a second time on the same step and starts from a fresh reference if TOFF stops. A malformed TOFF (a field of the four missing, fractional, negative or out of range) is a parse error and never enters the ring. With a remote gpsd (`--gps-allow-remote`), `clock` is that host's clock, so a step of it reads as a TOFF step; on loopback, gpsd's clock is this host's and its step is taken out.

**Read time, the fallback** for a fix with no TOFF near it (a gpsd that sends none, a receiver gpsd times no cycle for): everything below, unchanged from 2026-11-09. `rule_gnss_time_jump()` (src/alerts/rules_gnss.c:695) walks the positioned-fix ring in `s->gnss_hist`. Each fix carries the receiver's `time` (unix ms), the host's wall and monotonic clocks at receipt, `leapseconds`, and `reacq` (src/gps.c). For each new fix `b` it computes the receiver's step against the host's **monotonic** clock from the **anchor** `a`, the last fix whose time was accepted, `(rx_b - rx_a) - (mono_b - mono_a)`, and the host's own wall step, `(wall_b - mono_b) - (wall_a - mono_a)`.

`mono` is when sloth read the TPV, not when the receiver produced it, and read latency only ever makes a fix look late. Four guards follow from that:

- the step must **hold on the next fix** (within half the threshold), so one late or wild TPV followed by one on time is not a moved clock;
- a fix **read from a queue** - the next fix is more than `GNSS_BACKLOG_MS` (250 ms) further on in the receiver's time than in the host's, as when a stalled reader (the TUI stopped with Ctrl-Z, a loaded host) drains what gpsd buffered a millisecond apart - is not judged; at a raised threshold its 1 s steps back are under the hold's half;
- a fix **read late** (#182) is not judged: summed over the reads since the anchor on which the receiver kept its fix interval (the median of its own time between consecutive fixes in the ring, within half the threshold), the host's gaps ran more than `GNSS_BACKLOG_MS` (250 ms) past the receiver's. A pair adds only what the host's gap shows on its own: the lesser of its excess over the receiver's gap and over the interval, nothing when those disagree in sign, so a receiver nudged a few hundred ms inside the interval while its fixes are read on time cannot make the rule take it for a late reader (the first draft did, and a spoofer could blind the rule with one nudge and have a step after it re-anchored on unjudged). It is the queued read's mirror. A reader late by a steady amount on every fix passes the queue test, since each next fix is no further on in receiver time than in host time, and the hold then confirms the lag: a reader starved 1.5 s reads as a held -1500 ms step and its recovery as a held +1500 ms one. The sum runs from the anchor because only the read where the reader fell behind shows a long gap. A real step at the ordinary cadence is untouched: its host gap is the interval and its receiver gap is off it by at least the threshold. A lateness standing 30 s (`GNSS_LATE_REBASE_MS`), or whose anchor has left the fix ring, is re-anchored on unjudged, so a reader that is permanently slower does not blind the rule; a real step inside such a run is absorbed with it. The rule is blind while a lateness stands, and after a re-anchored lag clears it is blind up to 30 s more, the anchor then being the late one. At 5 Hz the 64-fix ring spans 12.8 s, so the anchor leaves it and the re-anchor comes sooner. Not caught: a reader that falls behind on the very pair where the receiver also dropped an epoch; that pair is off the interval, and must be, because at 1 Hz a dropped epoch and a whole-second forward step have the same receiver gap;
- none of these becomes the anchor, so each fix after a run of them is measured from the last good one. The first version measured the pair after an outlier from the fix before it, itself an outlier inside a drained queue, and fired on a four-second stall.

A host **suspend** stops `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and neither the receiver's clock nor the host's wall clock: when both moved past it together, forward, by at least the threshold (to half of it), the fix is a reacquisition.

- **Fires** when `|step| >= gnss.time_jump_ms` from the anchor, the fix was not read late, and the step holds on the next fix, which is not a queued read. The rule waits one fix for that confirmation; the fired fix becomes the anchor.
- **Not judged**: a fix that is a reacquisition (first fix since start, a mode 0/1 TPV, a lost source or a suspend: "compare only fix to fix"); a fix without the receiver's time; and the shapes `time_step_benign()` names - a whole-second step (±250 ms) equal to minus the change in `leapseconds`, up to `leapseconds` seconds when the count first appears after a cold boot, a one-second step within 2 s of UTC midnight, and a step within 60 s of ±1 or ±2 × 1024 weeks.
- **Key** `gnsstime`. **Severity** WARN. **Detail**: the step; the host's gap between the two reads, the receiver's gap between their times, and the fix interval, so a late read (reads past the interval, receiver on it) is told from a step (reads on the interval, receiver off it); the clock offset before and after, the host clock step, and the two `leapseconds` values. A host clock step alone (NTP, `date -s`) moves the offset and never fires this. Judged on TOFF, the detail carries instead the step, gpsd's receiver-minus-clock offset before and after, the receiver time between the two TOFFs, the host clock step and the two `leapseconds` values.

Empty technique, second exemption form, with a second reason: the step comes from faults as well as attacks (research/gnss/attack-mapping.md).

## The attack

Psiaki & Humphreys §III-C: "If the spoofer causes the receiver clock error to change too rapidly, then the victim receiver can detect that the rate of clock drift is larger than is reasonable" [4]; §II-D: a stealthy attack "should not attempt to alter the victim's position or timing fix too rapidly. Otherwise, the attack may be recognized by the unphysical nature of the changes" [4]. Humphreys et al. 2008 would "flag jumps of more than, say, 100 ns" [4] - a receiver-internal figure. Through gpsd over USB with no PPS, the in-band delay "may be stable to 10 mSec for hours and then jump by 200mSec ... you can't expect accuracy to UTC much better than 1 second from this method" [2]: hence the 200 ms floor and the 1000 ms default.

Why it matters on this host: a receiver feeding chrony through a refclock marked `trust` - "Assume time from this source is always true" - outvotes every NTP server [1]. chrony's own guards (`makestep` limited to boot, `maxchange`) already refuse a large step after start-up; the exposure is `trust` or a slow pull within the slew limits, "exactly the patient drag-off" the spoofing literature describes [1]. This rule catches the impatient step, not the patient pull, which needs comparison against independent NTP sources.

## Evidence that proves it

What sloth records: the alert detail; `gnss_fix` records carry `rx_time_ms`, `time_off_ms` and `leap`, signed on the clock-offset band held with hysteresis, so a multi-second step writes a record (docs/wiki/gnss.md); `sensor_health.gnss_time_offset_ms`; with `--db`, `gnss_fixes.time_off_ms`. gpsd's TOFF offset is not recorded outside the alert detail: the rule reads it from the in-memory ring, and no JSONL field or sloth.db column was added for it (2026-11-12).

A moved clock looks like: the receiver's time stepped against the monotonic clock and stayed stepped, the host clock did not step, `leapseconds` did not change, the date is not off by 1024 weeks, and the receiver did not restart. Compare against the host's NTP sources: if they agree with each other and not with the receiver, the receiver is the outlier.

## Benign causes

- A leap second, or the leap count arriving after a cold boot ("reported UTC time may be slightly inaccurate between a cold boot or leap second and the following subframe broadcast") [2] - excluded.
- A week-number rollover fault, "a date error of a multiple of 1024 weeks" [3] - excluded, and a fault for the receiver's manufacturer.
- A cold start: "must search the full time and frequency space" [1] - not judged.
- USB and gpsd latency, which jitters by up to a few hundred milliseconds [2] - under the floor.

Excluded and tested (tests/test_gnss_rules.c): a host clock step; a one-minute suspend; a four-second reader stall draining four queued TPVs; a six-second stall under a 3000 ms threshold; a reader 1.5 s late over six fixes that then catches up, judged at once and tick by tick (#182); a reader whose lateness wanders 0.8..2.2 s from fix to fix, and one jittering 1.4..1.6 s, judged at once and tick by tick (#182, 2026-11-12); the first fix after a loss; a leap second; the leap count arriving; a one-second step at UTC midnight; a 1024-week rollover; a step that does not hold; 800 ms under the default. Also tested: a backward step at the ordinary cadence fires, with both gaps and the fix interval in the detail; a reader that stays 1.5 s late is re-anchored on after 30 s and a real step after that fires; a 5 s step after a wandering lag has cleared fires; a receiver nudged 300 ms either way with its fixes read on time, then stepped 5 s, still fires; the rule waits for the next fix; and `c` does not replay a judged jump. On TOFF (hand-built gpsd TPV, TOFF and PPS lines through src/gps.c's parser and feed, TOFF on every other TPV, 2026-11-12): a stable TOFF under a reader that falls 1.5 s behind on the pair where the receiver dropped an epoch, the read-time measure's blind spot, does not fire, and the same stream without TOFF does (the control); a reader 1.3..1.7 s late over six fixes with a PPS line in the stream does not fire; a +5 s receiver step that holds fires once naming TOFF; a -3 s step under a 1.2 s reader lag fires; a TOFF step that does not hold, a host clock step and a one-minute suspend do not fire; when TOFF stops, a step is judged on read time and says so. Each absence seen red under its mutation, named in the test.

## Assessment

**Severity.** WARN is right: actionable for a host taking time from the receiver, and ambiguous between a fault and an attack.

**Default.** 1000 ms because "anything below roughly a second is noise on its own terms" for in-band time over USB, with the HOWTO's 200 ms jump as the floor the knob cannot go under [1][2].

**Sufficient to act on?** Sufficient to take the receiver out of time selection until explained; not sufficient to assert spoofing.

**First step for a responder.** Check whether chrony selects this receiver (`chronyc sources`) and how it is marked; then compare its time with the NTP sources.
