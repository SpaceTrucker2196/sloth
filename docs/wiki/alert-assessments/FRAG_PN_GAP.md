## What the rule checks

Input: the fragment-session table in `src/fragattack.c`, fed on every data frame from `src/capture/probe.c:535`. For every fragmented data frame (`fragmented = fn > 0 || more`, `:488`) `frag_track()` (`:320`) keys a session on `(BSSID, SA, DA, TID)` (`sess_key_t`, `:108-111`; TID 0 for non-QoS frames, `dot11_frame_tid`, `src/dot11_frame.c:212-216`). Fragment number 0 opens or replaces the session (`:324-327`; `sess_open`, `:289-309`), recording the CCMP packet number as `last_pn` (`:305`), the fragment number as `last_fn` (`:306`) and the **sequence number** as `seq_ctrl` (`:307`, `sc & 0xfff0`). The PN comes from `dot11_frame_ccmp_pn()` (`src/dot11_frame.c:223-238`): -1 unless the frame is Protected, has eight body bytes, and the KeyID octet carries the Extended IV bit (`:231`), so WEP and original-TKIP IVs are never read as a PN; the six PN octets are assembled in the standard's split order (`:233-238`).

Condition on a continuation (`src/fragattack.c:374-399`), all of:

- both the continuation and the previous fragment carry a readable PN (`pn >= 0 && s->last_pn >= 0`, `:389`);
- `fn > s->last_fn` (`:389`) - a repeat of the same fragment is not compared;
- the continuation's **sequence number equals the session's** (`:390`) - this branch, alone in the file, binds the two fragments to one MSDU;
- `pn - last_pn != fn - last_fn` (`:391-393`).

Then `pn_gap++` and `last_sa/last_da/last_hit` (`:394-397`). The delta comparison, not a bare `+1`, is what makes a fragment sloth did not hear (both steps 2) quiet and a retransmission (both steps 0, skipped by `fn > last_fn`) quiet (`:379-385`). After the comparison, `last_pn` and `last_fn` are updated **whether or not the sequence number matched** (`:400-401`), and the session closes on `!more` regardless (`:404`) - see the code-level benign cause below.

`rule_fragattack()` fires `ALERT_TYPE_FRAG_PN_GAP` at CRIT when `pn_gap > 0` (`src/alerts/rules_twin.c:624-632`). Key `fragpn:<BSSID>` (`:625`). Detail `"%u encrypted reassembl%s on %.17s with non-consecutive packet numbers - last %.17s -> %.17s (CVE-2020-26146)"` (`:626-629`). `match_ip` NULL, `match_port` 0 (`:630-631`): no per-alert pcap (`src/alert_pcap.c:105`). Resolution is 300 s after the last re-assert (`include/sloth.h:860`), in practice only on BSS-row eviction (`src/fragattack.c:47-48`) or restart. `last_sa`/`last_da` are one pair per BSS row shared by every FragAttacks branch.

Two documents in the tree still say this detector does not exist: `src/fragattack.h:134-147` ("CVE-2020-26146 ... is NOT here") and the corpus document's "Related" note [1, line 116]. Both predate the code at `src/fragattack.c:374-399`; `docs/wiki/fragattacks.md:116-168` records why the objections were withdrawn and is the current account. The header and corpus notes should be updated.

## The mechanism

Background: "all fragments of a specific frame have the same 12-bit sequence number", "Only unicast data frames are (de)fragmented", and "to prevent an adversary from forging a frame by combining fragments of different frames, a receiver must drop all fragments if their PNs are not consecutive" [2, Section 2.2]; the Linux receiver quotes the clause: "The receiver shall discard MSDUs and MMPDUs whose constituent MPDU PN values are not incrementing in steps of 1" (IEEE 802.11 12.5.3.4.4 item d for CCMP, 12.5.5.4.4 for GCMP) and enforces it by requiring the next fragment's PN to equal the previous plus one [10]. CVE-2020-26146 is receivers that skip that check: "devices do not check whether all fragments of a frame have consecutive packet numbers, i.e., whether the received fragments indeed belong to the same frame" [2, Section 6.2]; CVE record: "reassemble fragments with non-consecutive packet numbers. An adversary can abuse this to exfiltrate selected fragments ... exploitable when another device sends fragmented frames and the WEP, CCMP, or GCMP data-confidentiality protocol is used. Note that WEP is vulnerable to this attack by design" [3].

The attack (Figure 9): a multi-channel MitM forwards the first fragment of an attacker-destined packet, `Enc^n_k{Frag0(s)}`, to the AP; later, when the client sends a packet with sensitive data as `Frag0(s0)`/`Frag1(s0)`, the adversary forwards the second half relabelled as `Frag1(s)` with its real PN n+d+1; a receiver that does not check PNs reassembles the two and sends the user data to the attacker's server; "Between stages 1 and 2, the attacker never uses sequence number s in forwarded frames" [2, Appendix B]. It is the same exfiltration as the mixed-key attack without needing a rekey, which is why the paper says the lack of this check means "our attacks are possible without relying on rekeys" [2, Section 4.7].

The on-air footprint is exactly what sloth keys on: same `(BSSID, SA, DA, TID)`, same sequence number s, fragment 0 then fragment 1, PN step d+1 against fragment step 1. What sloth cannot know is whether the AP accepted the pair - a patched receiver drops the second fragment [10] and the alert still fires, because the forwarded frames were transmitted either way.

Tooling: `ping I,E,E --inc-pn 2` ("Send a fragmented ping with non-consecutive packet numbers"); the README notes the test "only failed against Linux and against devices that don't support fragmentation" [5].

## Evidence that proves it

What sloth records: `alert`/`alert.create` JSONL under `fragpn:<BSSID>` with the detail; the sloth.db `alerts` row; the `[c]` view row with `pn_gap`, the pair, age and gate count (`docs/views/fragattack.md`). No per-alert pcap and no evidence-frame IDs.

What makes it real: on the named BSSID, for one `(SA, DA, TID)` and one sequence number, two Protected fragments (Extended IV set) with fragment numbers a < b whose CCMP PNs differ by other than b - a, with More Fragments set on the first. In the attack the first fragment's plaintext is the start of an IP packet to the attacker's server and the second is the victim's own data, so a transmitter whose MAC is the victim's but whose radio is the adversary's; the gap d is however many frames the victim sent in between on the rogue channel [2, Appendix B]. An external capture on the AP's channel shows the same two frames.

What refutes it: the two fragments came from a transmitter that assigns PNs non-consecutively across its own fragment burst (a stack bug, not a MitM: fingerprint is the same gap on every fragmented MSDU from that station, with sane payloads); or the code-level artefact below, whose fingerprint is a third fragmented MSDU on the same tuple, with a different sequence number, heard between the session's fragment 0 and the counted continuation.

## Benign causes

- **Non-consecutive PN assignment by a transmitter.** The clause binds the receiver's check to the transmitter's "steps of 1" [10], and the paper's test tool has a `--pn-per-qos` option for targets that only accept consecutive PNs, implying some stacks keep per-queue counters [5]; a transmitter that does not keep its fragment PNs consecutive is a conformance bug rather than an attack. Fingerprint: every fragmented MSDU from that station shows the same shape; `pn_gap` climbs steadily, not once.
- **Interleaved continuation of another MSDU on the same tuple (code-level).** `last_pn`/`last_fn` are updated even when the sequence number did not match (`src/fragattack.c:400-401`), and `!more` closes the session either way (`:404`). So: fragment 0 of MSDU A opens the session; a continuation of MSDU B (fragment 0 missed, same tuple) arrives, is not counted (`:390` fails) but overwrites `last_pn`/`last_fn` and may close the session; a later fragment of A then compares against B's PN. Needs a missed fragment 0 (a hopping radio) and two fragmented MSDUs in flight on one tuple. Fix: guard `:400-401` with the same sequence test.
- **A retransmission** is excluded by `fn > last_fn` (`:389`) - same PN, same FN - so a congested link does not fire this.
- **A rekey mid-reassembly** restarts PNs, so a continuation after a rekey has a negative step and fires here as well as `FRAG_MIXKEY`; the pair firing together on one BSS is one event, not two.

## Assessment

CRIT fits: two fragments bound by sequence number whose PNs do not advance with their fragment numbers is the attack's footprint, the standard forbids the shape, and sloth's version of the check is the receiver's own with the hopping-radio case handled by deltas (`docs/wiki/fragattacks.md:116-168`). This is the best-grounded of the four session-table rules because it is the only one that uses the sequence number. Two gaps limit it: first, sloth holds **one** session per `(BSSID, SA, DA, TID)` and replaces it on any fragment 0 (`src/fragattack.c:285-288`, `:324-327`), while the standard requires receivers to hold at least three concurrent reassemblies [2, Section 4.3], so an adversary who also forwards the victim's new fragment 0 displaces the session and the later `Frag1(s)` finds no match - the attack in Figure 9 avoids that only because the attacker chooses not to forward it; second, the `:400-401` update on a non-matching sequence number can manufacture a gap from two interleaved MSDUs. Both are cheap to close (a small per-tuple ring keyed by sequence number; a sequence guard on the update).

Prevalence: 52 of 68 fragmentation-capable devices in 2021, with Linux-kernel reassembly and two Intel/Windows combinations the only ones that checked [2, Section 6.2]; five years of patching since (62 advisory links [9]); CVSS 5.3 NVD, 4.8 Cisco [3][6]; no in-the-wild use reported [4][7]. The attack needs a multi-channel MitM and a network that fragments, which the paper notes is uncommon unless 802.11ax dynamic fragmentation is in play [2, Section 4.7].

Reference host: not seen (null). If it fires, the count will be ticks since the first hit, not fragments; read the detail integer, and expect the `FRAG_MIXKEY` co-fire if the gap came from a rekey.

Sufficient to act on alone? For triage, yes: it is a well-bound frame pair. For attribution, confirm from an external capture that the two same-sequence fragments came from a radio that is not the station's (RSSI, timing, retry behaviour), and that the gap is not a per-MSDU constant for that station (stack bug). Then patch the receiver on that BSS against the vendor advisory [9], per `docs/wiki/alert-remediation.md:1171-1185` - noting that page's investigate step still carries the pre-implementation argument that PN gaps are weak, which `docs/wiki/fragattacks.md:116-168` has since answered. No tuning knob exists.
