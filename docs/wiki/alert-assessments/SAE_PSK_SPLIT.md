## What the rule checks

Input: `s->beacon_aps` (the parsed beacon table, each row carrying `ssid`, `bssid`, `akm_bits` from the RSN element) and `s->assoc_reqs` (observed association requests with their `akm_bits`). The rule (src/alerts/rules_twin.c:751-808) walks the beacon table from the SAE side:

- outer loop: an AP that is SAE-family and not PSK-family — `(a->akm_bits & RSN_AKM_SAE_FAMILY) && !(a->akm_bits & RSN_AKM_PSK_FAMILY)` (756-758), skipping hidden SSIDs (754);
- inner loop: a different BSSID (764) with the same SSID (763) that is PSK-family and not SAE-family (765-766).

The families are bitmasks over whole AKM groups: `RSN_AKM_SAE_FAMILY` = SAE | FT-SAE | SAE-EXT | FT-SAE-EXT and `RSN_AKM_PSK_FAMILY` the PSK group (src/rsn_ie.h:50-57), so FT and SAE-EXT variants count, not just suites 8 and 2.

Two modifiers set severity:

- **taken** (772-780): an association request recorded against the PSK-only BSSID that names PSK and not SAE — a client that actually took the weak lane.
- **diff_oui** (785): the two BSSIDs' first three MAC octets differ, i.e. different vendor, so it is not the same physical unit's other radio.

Fire (802-804): `ALERT_TYPE_SAE_PSK_SPLIT` at `(taken || diff_oui) ? CRIT : WARN`. Key: built with the typed `akey_*` builder — `akey_begin(..., "saesplit")` then `akey_remote(&k, a->ssid, 32)` (793-796), the SSID being a remote-controlled string sanitised and hashed whole (#112); if the key overflows the rule breaks rather than firing a merge-prone empty key. Detail: `"'%.16s' is SAE-only on %s and PSK-only on %s%s%s"` with ` - different vendor` and/or ` - a client associated with PSK` appended (797-801).

This is deliberately distinct from WPA_DOWNGRADE (one AP offering both families = transition mode, #62) and from EVIL_TWIN (whose CRIT branch needs a weak OPEN/WEP enc and whose WARN branch needs the same cipher) — the comment at 729-750 explains it fills the gap between EVIL_TWIN's two branches. It does not cover one BSSID changing SAE->PSK over time; that is SAE_PSK_REGRESSION.

## The attack

WPA3-Personal transition mode lets an AP accept both WPA3 (SAE) and WPA2 (PSK) connections under one SSID and password, so WPA3 clients get SAE while legacy clients fall back to PSK [3, §2.3]. Dragonblood showed this is a downgrade primitive: an adversary broadcasts a WPA2-only network with the target SSID; a client that supports WPA3 but is willing to use WPA2 connects over PSK to the rogue AP, performs the WPA2 4-way handshake, and the adversary — who can forge the unauthenticated message 1 — captures the authenticated message 2, which is enough material for an offline dictionary attack on the PSK [3, §4.1.1][5]. The paper found iwd and the Samsung Galaxy S10 vulnerable even against WPA3-only target networks [3, §4.1.1]. The recommended client defence is trust-on-first-use: "after successfully connecting using SAE, the client should store that the network supports SAE" and refuse a later downgrade [3, §4.1.3]. (The Wi-Fi Alliance's later Transition Disable indication in the WPA3 Specification is the standardised form of this; that document was not fetched here, so the sentence is stated from memory and unverified.) CERT/CC VU#871675 is the Dragonblood advisory, but it covers the hostapd/wpa_supplicant implementation CVEs (CVE-2019-9494..9499) and does not itself discuss the transition-mode downgrade [4]; the repo corpus cites it for this type regardless [1].

SAE_PSK_SPLIT is what that downgrade looks like on the air when the two lanes are offered by *different BSSIDs* under one SSID rather than by one AP in transition mode: an SAE-only BSSID (the real network) beside a PSK-only BSSID (the rogue, offering the lane it can actually crack). The repo maps the PSK-only half to T1557.004 Evil Twin — "the PSK-only half of a split SSID is the lane an evil twin wants a client to take" (include/sloth_alerts.def:115; research/mitre/T1557-004.md:26) [9]; MITRE's own T1557.004 page describes evil twins generically and does not mention WPA3 lanes [6]. Evil-twin tooling that does this includes wifiphisher (evil twin + deauth + phishing) and the Wi-Fi Pineapple used by APT28 in MITRE's own procedure example [6][11]. A genuine dual-band router mid-migration produces the same two beacons without any attacker, which is why severity is gated on OUI difference and on a client actually taking the lane.

## Evidence that proves it

What sloth records: `alert`/`alert.create` JSONL with the `saesplit:<sanitised SSID>:<hash>` key (`saesplit:` + the SSID sanitised and cut to 32 bytes + `:` + 16-hex FNV-1a of the whole SSID, per src/alert_key.h:21-22), the detail naming both BSSIDs and whether a client associated with PSK, and severity WARN or CRIT. The beacon rows behind it persist to the `--db` `beacon_aps`/`ssid_akm_history` tables (src/db.c:266-270) and the association requests to `assoc_reqs`. No per-alert pcap (`match_ip = NULL`, rules_twin.c:802-804). The alert is an inference from two beacons (confidence via `fire_conf` is 0 here because `fire()` is used — the twin family's confidence hook is not invoked for this rule).

To call it real: two BSSIDs beaconing one SSID, one advertising only SAE in its RSN AKM list and the other only PSK, present at the same time; and ideally an association request to the PSK-only BSSID naming PSK. The strong confirmers that point at an attacker rather than a migration: the PSK-only BSSID's vendor OUI differs from the SAE-only one (the CRIT trigger), its signal is anomalously strong or appears/disappears with deauth activity against the real BSSID, and it is not in the operator's inventory. Cross-check the `[v]` alerts view and the Wi-Fi AP view for the two BSSIDs' channels, signal and first-seen times.

What would refute it (benign fingerprint): both BSSIDs share a vendor OUI and sit on different bands of one physical AP (a dual-band unit configured inconsistently — real finding, but WARN, not an intrusion); or the operator confirms a staged WPA3 migration is in progress with one radio cut over. A transition-mode AP (both families on one BSSID) is explicitly not this alert — that is WPA_DOWNGRADE.

## Benign causes

- **Staged WPA3 migration.** An ESS where some APs/radios have been moved to SAE-only and others are still PSK-only produces a genuine split. Documented as the expected transition path; the paper itself proposes "a flag ... added to the RSNE that indicates only some APs of a network support WPA3" for exactly this state [3, §4.1.3]. Fingerprint: operator-owned BSSIDs, often same vendor, stable over time, no deauth activity, no unexpected strong-signal BSSID.
- **Dual-band unit misconfigured** with SAE on one band and PSK on the other. Same vendor OUI -> WARN by design. Fingerprint: the two BSSIDs differ only in the band-specific bits of the MAC and share a channel-pair.
- **Separate WPA2 and WPA3 networks that happen to share an SSID string** (e.g. a guest SSID reused). Fingerprint: different passwords, different infrastructure, operator can confirm.

## Assessment

Severity: the WARN/CRIT design is sound. WARN for an in-vendor split (migration or misconfig) and CRIT when the OUI differs or a client took the PSK lane is exactly the line between "a network posture worth noting" and "a client has been moved onto the crackable lane". The caveat is that a legitimate mixed-vendor migration can reach CRIT; the "a client associated with PSK" modifier is the better CRIT trigger of the two, since OUI difference alone is satisfied by a multi-vendor ESS.

Prevalence: the transition-mode downgrade is a demonstrated, named WPA3 weakness [3][5] and evil-twin rogue APs offering a weak lane are a staple of Wi-Fi attack tooling [6][11]; MITRE records APT28 using Wi-Fi Pineapple evil twins [9]. But the two-BSSID split specifically is most often a migration artefact in benign networks, so a sighting is a prompt to investigate, not a confirmed intrusion.

Noise on the reference host: not seen (host:null). The per-tick-per-SSID re-fire inflates `count` the way the FRAG_* rules do — a single standing split produces one fire per tick for as long as both beacons are in the table. Recommend keying the fire on the tuple set `(SSID, SAE-BSSID, PSK-BSSID, taken)` and emitting only on change, and resolving when SAE returns to the PSK-only BSSID or the PSK-only BSSID disappears. A noisy deployment mid-migration should pin its own BSSIDs via `--my-bssid` and have the rule suppress WARN on owned pairs (not currently wired).

Sufficient to act on? For WARN, no action beyond noting the migration. For CRIT with "a client associated with PSK", yes — that is a client on the crackable lane and the responder should: confirm whether the PSK-only BSSID is the operator's; if not, locate it (signal-strength direction-finding from the `[v]`/AP views) and remove it or report it to the site; and advise affected clients to forget-and-reconnect so a WPA3-capable client re-pins SAE. The CVE/mechanism is Dragonblood; the split itself is configuration or a rogue, so the "fix" is not a patch but moving the PSK lane to SAE or removing the rogue [2].
