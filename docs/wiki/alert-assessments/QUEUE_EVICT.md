## What the rule checks

Two stages, two threads.

**Capture thread (`src/queue_evict.c`).** A row is created per (BSSID, STA) only by a session change: `queue_evict_note_session_change_ev()` (queue_evict.c:63) is called from `src/capture/probe.c:622` on a status-0 Association/Reassociation Response (`QUEUE_EVICT_SRC_ASSOC`/`_REASSOC`) and from `probe.c:657` on a Disassociation/Deauthentication (`_DISCONNECT`). A group-addressed station (broadcast deauth) creates nothing (queue_evict.c:72). Each change stamps `event_us` from the frame's own capture clock in microseconds, resets the pending burst and sets `burst_last_seq = -1` (queue_evict.c:77-90).

Downlink frames are then counted in `queue_evict_observe_downlink_frame()` (queue_evict.c:103) only if: type 2 data (106); FromDS set and ToDS clear, i.e. AP->STA, excluding uplink and four-address WDS (112); Retry bit clear (118); not Null/QoS-Null (123); DA not group-addressed (141); a row already exists for the pair (146); the frame is stamped at or after `event_us` and within `queue_evict.window_ms` (default 100 ms, queue_evict.h:57; `--tune` range 1..10000, tune.c:212) of it (155-157); and its sequence number differs from the last counted one (163-168). When `burst_frames` reaches exactly `queue_evict.min_frames` (default 3, queue_evict.h:62; range 2..1000, tune.c:213), `release_count` increments and `last_release_ts`/`last_release_src` are stamped (179-183); every frame at or past the threshold updates `last_release_frames` and `last_release_span_ms` (the burst's own span) and rewrites `release_ids` with the session-change frame id plus the newest burst ids (184-199). `queue_evict.report_secs` defaults to 900 (queue_evict.h:74; range 1..86400, tune.c:214).

**Rule tick (`rule_queue_evict()`, rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1166).** Snapshots the table; skips rows with `release_count == 0` (1172) and rows whose `last_release_ts` is older than `queue_evict.report_secs` (1189-1191) - the recency bound exists because `release_count` is lifetime, and without it one roam would fire for the rest of the run and 128 roaming clients would fill `MAX_ALERTS` (also 128). Corroboration (1196-1206): `pm_forge_lookup()` for the same STA with `contradiction_count > 0` and `|last_contradiction_ts - last_release_ts| <= 10 s` (`QUEUE_EVICT_CORROB_SECS`, 1164); absolute difference because a hopping radio may hear the two out of order. Severity ladder (1209-1211): uncorroborated -> LOW/25; corroborated -> WARN/70; corroborated and `ownership_is_my_bssid()` -> CRIT/70. Ownership alone never escalates (1152-1162).

Key `qevict:<sta>@<bssid>` (1218). Detail names the station, BSSID, the burst (N frames in span ms), the session-change type, `x<release_count>`, and either the corroborated Framing-Frames wording or the uncorroborated wording naming the benign readings; ` - YOUR network` when owned (1219-1241). Fired via `fire_inv()` with the confidence and `release_ids` (up to 8 evidence-ring ids, session-change frame first), no `match_ip` (1246-1249).

Count semantics from the engine: `fire_inv()` on an existing unresolved key does `evaluations++; count = evaluations` (engine.c:478-479; `alert_t.evaluations` = "fire() calls - rule ticks", sloth.h:1042). `observations` moves only when the detail signature or severity changes (engine.c:487-488), which here means a new release or the corroboration flipping. Resolves after `ALERT_RESOLVE_AFTER_S` = 300 s with no re-assertion (sloth.h:860), i.e. ~1,200 s after the last release.

## The attack

The alert's basis is CVE-2022-47522, "Framing Frames," by Schepers, Ranganathan (Northeastern) and Vanhoef (KU Leuven), USENIX Security 2023 [1][5]. The design flaw is in the 802.11 standard itself: an access point decides whether to hold a station's downlink traffic purely from the Power Management bit in that station's frame header, and that bit sits in Frame Control, which is never encrypted or authenticated [5]. The standard "does not define any behavior for how an access point should manage its transmit queues when the security context changes" [5], so buffered frames can survive a change of key. NVD states the net effect: the specifications "do not require an access point to purge its transmit queue before removing a client's pairwise encryption key," letting a physically proximate attacker intercept target-destined frames by combining a spoofed source address, power-save frames, and frames that remove the target's security context; CVSS 3.1 7.5 (AV:A/AC:H/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H) [1].

The paper describes two families. In the queue-leak family (paper §3), buffered frames are released after the station's key has been torn down, so a vulnerable driver emits them in plaintext, under the group key, or under an all-zero key; the authors confirmed plaintext, WEP-all-zero and CCMP-group-key outcomes across FreeBSD/Ralink/Realtek drivers (Table 1) and against Linux+hostapd/IWD [5]. In the security-context-override family (paper §5), an attacker who can authenticate to the AP under the victim's MAC in a hotspot-like network (eduroam, Passpoint, SAE-PK) causes the AP to associate the attacker's own session keys with the victim's address; the authors confirmed this on LANCOM, Aruba and hostapd, and noted it fails where a cached PMK is shared across BSSIDs (Cisco Catalyst 9130) [5, Table 2]. MITRE T1557 (Adversary-in-the-Middle) is the family sloth tags; the technique assignment is the repo's, matching the paper's AiTM framing. The relevant tooling is the authors' own MacStealer reference implementation of the §5 override attack [7].

What sloth sees is only the tail of this: a session-change frame for one pair, then a downlink burst. It cannot see the key the frames went out under, cannot confirm a MAC was spoofed, and cannot see whether any interception happened - which is exactly why the rule leans entirely on PM_FORGE corroboration for anything above LOW.

## Evidence that proves it

For each release the row records: the station and BSSID, the session-change source (`association` / `reassociation` / `disassoc/deauth`), `last_release_frames` (frames counted in the burst), `last_release_span_ms` (how fast they arrived, first to last counted frame), and `release_count` (lifetime qualifying bursts). The detail renders all of these.

A per-alert pcap is **not** written on the match_ip/port path - there is no IP flow, so `match_ip` is NULL. Instead the alert carries up to 8 evidence-ring event IDs (`release_ids`): the session-change frame first, then the newest frames of the released burst (src/queue_evict.c:191-199; docs/wiki/pcap-export.md §4, the `QUEUE_EVICT` row at line 149, confirms the citation shape). Those bytes reach disk only behind `--collect-handshakes` into `--eapol-dir` (#92). JSONL carries `evidence_count` and `evidence_id_0..7` (docs/wiki/jsonl-schema.md). `sensor_health.evict_queue_evict` moves when the 128-row table evicts the stalest pair.

To call a release real rather than a roam, a reader needs the one thing the burst alone cannot give: corroboration. That is a PM_FORGE contradiction for the **same station** within 10 s - a PM=1 Null/QoS-Null and a PM=0 data frame claiming one source address inside the AP's own beacon interval, which one honest half-duplex radio cannot produce. In the evidence frames that means the session-change frame should be a status-0 (re)association response or a deauth/disassoc, and the burst should be unicast AP->STA data frames with distinct sequence numbers packed into <=100 ms. What refutes it: the absence of any PM_FORGE on that station (the ordinary-roam fingerprint - a client that simply reassociated and received its pending traffic), a span close to the full window rather than a tight drain, and a `release_count` that climbs once per genuine roam rather than in rapid succession.

## Benign causes

- **Ordinary roaming.** The dominant false positive and the reason the default is LOW: an AP that has just admitted a station has queued traffic for it, so a reassociation followed by a downlink burst is the single most common event on any busy BSS (docs/wiki/alerts.md:317-335, §"When the observable itself is ambiguous (#107 shape B)").
- **Buffered-traffic flush after a legitimate wake.** A real power-save client waking and draining its DTIM-buffered unicast traffic looks the same from outside.
- **Fast BSS transition / 802.11r roams** between APs of one ESS produce reassociation responses and immediate downlink bursts by design.

The discriminator is PM_FORGE, not anything in the burst itself; the rule is explicit that the uncorroborated detail must not use the words attack or attacker.

## Assessment

Severity is correct and unusually well-reasoned: the entire ladder rests on corroboration, not on the observable, because the observable is ambiguous by construction (docs/wiki/alerts.md:317 ff.). On the reference host - 3 keys, 78,873 fires, all LOW - the arithmetic says the rule counts per tick while a release stays within the 900 s report window, so ~26,000 counts per key reflects a station whose releases (or whose long reconnect tail) kept a row "recent" for a large fraction of the run, not 26,000 attacks. Zero fires reached WARN, so PM_FORGE never corroborated: these are three roaming/reconnecting stations and the rule behaved exactly as designed by holding them at LOW. The noise is in the count column, not in the row count; the remedy is presentational (show `release_count`, not `count`) plus optionally a shorter `report_secs`. A responder should first check the device's other alerts for a PM_FORGE on the same station within seconds; with none, treat it as a roam (docs/wiki/alert-remediation.md §QUEUE_EVICT). Only the corroborated-on-your-own-network case (CRIT) warrants acting: patch the AP firmware to a build whose vendor issued a CVE-2022-47522 advisory.
