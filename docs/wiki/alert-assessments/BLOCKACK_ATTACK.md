## What the rule checks

Inputs. The per-triple Block-Ack state table in src/ctrl_frames.c, filled by `bar_observe()` (src/ctrl_frames.c:177-210) from Block-Ack Request frames (control subtype 8, `CTRL_SUB_BLOCKACK_REQ`). A BAR carries a transmitter (addr2, who it claims to be), a receiver (addr1, the peer being asked to advance its window), a TID, and a Block Ack Starting Sequence Control field whose top 12 bits are the Starting Sequence Number. State is keyed on (receiver, transmitter, TID) because traffic classes have independent sequence spaces; the table drops multi-TID BARs (BAR Control bit 1) and group-addressed RA/TA (ctrl_frames.c:183-192); the fragment-number bits are simply ignored. Per BAR it bumps `total_bars`, and - except for the first BAR of a triple, which establishes the baseline - computes the forward distance from the last SSN to this one and, if it exceeds `BAR_SUSPICIOUS_JUMP` (128), bumps `suspicious_bars` and records `last_jump` (ctrl_frames.c:200-205). Because the distance is modulo 4096, a *backward* step is a large forward one: an SSN one lower than the last reads as 4095. So a duplicate or reordered older BAR counts as suspicious, and after a spoofed forward jump the station's next genuine BAR (back at the real SSN) is counted too - one attack frame typically yields two suspicious BARs. Only BARs are examined: the paper's second variant, a spoofed Block Ack (subtype 9) blocking all of an AP's stations [3], is counted per source in the control table but is not tested by this rule.

The hazard the module exists for. SSN is a **12-bit field that wraps** every 4096 frames, which on a busy link is minutes. A jump computed as plain subtraction reads 4090->10 as a jump of 4080 instead of an advance of 16, firing constantly on healthy connections. `bar_ssn_forward_distance()` (src/ctrl_frames.c:140-146) computes `(to - from) & 4095`, the modulo-4096 forward distance, and is exposed so the wrap arithmetic is tested directly - "get this wrong and the detector is green in CI and a false-positive generator on the air" (ctrl_frames.h comment).

Condition. `rule_blockack_attack()` asks `bar_worst_offender(now, 300, ...)` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:356; ctrl_frames.c:211-223) for the triple with the largest cumulative `suspicious_bars` among triples whose `last_bar_ts` - updated by every BAR, suspicious or not - is inside the trailing 300 s; returns if none. It returns **one** triple per tick, so only that triple's key is re-asserted; every other triple with suspicious BARs is not evaluated that tick, and an incident whose triple stops being the worst offender resolves 300 s later even if it is still active - a noisy observation-gap triple therefore masks a genuine attack on another triple (ties keep the earlier slot, `<= best`, ctrl_frames.c:211-223). Nothing decays `suspicious_bars`: a triple with one historical jump re-qualifies on every tick for as long as it keeps sending any BARs, until its slot is evicted (`BAR_MAX_PAIRS` = 128, LRU). It then checks whether any channel's retry ratio is at or above `RF_RETRY_DEGRADED_PCT` (40%) as **corroboration, not a gate** (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:364-366): a stalled Block-Ack window usually shows up as sustained retries, but requiring it would make the rule depend on `rf_quality` having sampled the right channel at the right moment - "on a hopping sensor that is a coin flip." Key is `bl0ck:<rx>:<tx>:<tid>` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:374). Severity is CRIT when `ownership_is_my_bssid(rx)`, else WARN (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:382-383). Only the receiver is tested: a spoofed BAR impersonating a client toward the operator's AP (RA = the AP) is CRIT, but one impersonating the operator's AP toward a client (RA = the client, TA = the `--my-bssid` address) is WARN. The detail names tx->rx, the TID, the SSN jump size, the suspicious/total BAR counts, and appends "(channel retrying hard)" and "YOUR network" as they apply. No evidence IDs, no match_ip, no pcap.

## The attack

802.11n added Block Acknowledgment: instead of ACKing each QoS data frame, a station sends a burst and the recipient confirms the whole block with one BA frame carrying a bitmap. The agreement is set up with an ADDBA exchange; the recipient keeps a reorder buffer with a `WinStartB` pointer (the lowest sequence number not yet received). A Block-Ack **Request** (BAR) carrying a Starting Sequence Number tells the recipient to advance that window (IEEE 802.11-2020 §10.25) [4].

The Bl0ck attack - Chatzoglou, Kampourakis and Kambourakis, 2023, CVE-2022-32666 [3][5] - spoofs a BAR on behalf of a legitimate station, carrying an arbitrary Starting Sequence Number far ahead of the real traffic. Per §10.25.6.6.3, on receiving a BAR with `WinStartB < SSN < WinStartB + 2^11` and no protected block-ack agreement, the recipient sets `WinStartB = SSN` - so the spoofed jump advances the receive window past frames that are still queued, and everything in the skipped range is discarded. The connection stalls. The paper demonstrates two variants - blocking a single STA (spoofed BAR impersonating that STA) and blocking all STAs of an AP at once (spoofed BA, even with a random transmitter MAC) - and found "all but two APs" of its eight-AP testbed vulnerable to the first, across Broadcom, MediaTek, Qualcomm, Intel and Huawei chipsets. The authors stress it "can be mounted with inexpensive equipment, little effort, and a low level of expertise" using scapy and an off-the-shelf injection NIC.

Why it is hard to notice: "No deauthentication, no flood, nothing any rate-based rule watches for. What the operator observes is a client that has stopped working, with a clean deauth table and no obvious cause" [1]. The paper itself notes the attacks "will most probably fly under the radar... they would go unnoticed by the typical intrusion detection system, which typically focuses on management rather [than] control frames." Protected block ack (the PBAC flag) defends it, but the paper notes PBAC support is "practically non-existent" even in recent devices.

## Evidence that proves it

What sloth records. The alert detail carries tx->rx, the TID, the SSN jump magnitude (`last_jump`), the suspicious and total BAR counts for the triple, the optional "(channel retrying hard)" corroboration, and the "YOUR network" flag. The `channel_summary` JSONL record carries per-channel `ctrl_blockack` volume (#64). No pcap is written (control frames carry no IP flow and the rule passes no evidence IDs).

What makes it real. A BAR whose Starting Sequence Number jumps forward more than 128 frames (modulo the wrap) is the Bl0ck signature: normal traffic advances the window by small increments, so a > 128 forward jump is a window being forced, not a connection progressing. Several such BARs for one (rx, tx, TID) triple, with the receiving side visibly struggling (channel retry ratio >= 40%), is the full picture: the spoofed window advance plus the stalled-connection symptom. A responder should see, in the evidence, repeated large SSN jumps against a client that stopped passing traffic.

What refutes it. The sequence-wrap false positive - a station transmitting steadily through the 12-bit space - is removed by the modulo arithmetic, and the first BAR of a triple is excluded, so a sensor joining mid-flight does not misread the baseline. What is *not* removed is a gap in observation: the distance is measured between the last BAR sloth saw and this one, so if the sensor hopped away (or lost frames) while the station legitimately sent more than 128 frames, the next BAR seen is a "jump". On a `--hop` sensor that is the first thing to rule out - check whether the sensor was on the triple's channel between the two BARs, and whether `suspicious_bars` is 1 against a large `total_bars`. If the finding shows only a single suspicious BAR with no retry corroboration and the client kept working, treat it cautiously; a genuine attack is a repeated pattern against a connection that stalls.

## Benign causes

No legitimate *transmitter* behaviour produces a BAR whose Starting Sequence Number is more than 128 frames ahead of the frames it actually sent. The detector, though, compares against the last BAR *observed*, so the benign case is on the sensor side: a channel hop, a range fade or dropped frames spanning more than 128 of the station's frames makes the next legitimate BAR read as a jump. The sequence-space wrap, the historical false positive for this class of detector, is removed by the modulo-4096 arithmetic; the observation gap is not, and because `suspicious_bars` never decays, one such gap keeps the key asserting for as long as the triple stays active. A backward SSN step - a retransmitted or reordered older BAR, or two monitor radios delivering the same exchange out of order - also counts, since modulo 4096 a step of -1 reads as a jump of 4095. The nearest benign edge is a sensor misreading a multi-TID or fragmented BAR layout, which the parser skips rather than guesses at (ctrl_frames.c:183-187). The retry-burst corroboration can also be absent for benign reasons (the sensor was on another channel when the stall happened), which is exactly why it is reported and not required.

## Assessment

Severity rationale. WARN/CRIT on `--my-bssid` ownership is the right split for a finding sloth can see the attempt of but never the effect of (the window advance happens inside the victim's receiver). Making the retry-burst corroboration optional rather than a gate is the correct engineering call: requiring it would silently suppress real detections whenever the hopping sensor missed the right channel. The modulo-4096 arithmetic makes the detector trustworthy against wraps; it is not trustworthy against observation gaps, which is a tuning gap rather than a grading one.

Real-world prevalence. Bl0ck is a recent (2023), widely-applicable result with a CVE [3][5] and a low skill bar, but it needs crafted control frames (no point-and-click tool exists) and there is no public telemetry on in-the-wild use. Treat it as a credible, low-base-rate threat that conventional WIDS miss - which is the gap this rule fills.

Reference-host noise. 30,184,449 fires on **85 keys**, max_sev CRIT. This is the clearest case in the batch of per-tick counting inflating the picture: ~355,000 fires per key is one (rx, tx, TID) incident held open and re-qualifying for about a day on average (<= 24.7 h at >= 4 ticks/s), not 30 M distinct events. Because `bar_worst_offender()` returns a single triple, at most one BLOCKACK key is asserted per tick, so those per-key durations are sequential, not concurrent: 30,184,449 fires implies at least that many ticks, i.e. <= 2,096 h (~87 days) of poll time at 4 ticks/s. If the reference capture was shorter than that, the tick rate was well above 4/s or the counts span several sessions; the per-key "about a day" is an average, and the totals cannot all be one run at the default cadence. The honest finding count is **85** - 85 triples that showed a > 128 forward jump at least once; given the observation-gap false positive, treat that as an upper bound on Bl0ck signatures, not a count of them. Cadence caveat: the tick arithmetic assumes the default 250 ms poll with an idle keyboard. `poll_tick()` runs once per main-loop iteration (`poll_data()` at src/main.c:1628 calls it at :205) and the wait before it is cut short by any keypress or by `event_wake_signal()` on a new or escalated alert (src/alerts/engine.c:526, 608), so real ticks/s is >= 4 and every duration derived here is an upper bound; `--refresh-ms` changes the base. The 30 M is poll ticks, not alerts and not frames. The per-triple key already dedupes correctly, so the fix is presentational, not a threshold or dedupe change: surface the incident/key count (85) and first_seen/last_seen instead of `evaluations`/`count` on the alert page. `observations` is not the answer here: it follows the BAR rate, because the detail carries `total_bars`, which rises on every BAR from the qualifying triple, and the "(channel retrying hard)" suffix, which tests every channel's `retry_pct` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:364-366) and so is a sensor-wide flag that can flip between ticks; each change against the stored `detail_sig`, refreshed only on an emitted event (engine.c:487, 534), keeps `observations` near `evaluations`.

Is the evidence enough to act on? To raise an investigation, yes - a repeated large-SSN-jump BAR pattern against a stalling client is a strong Bl0ck signature; a single jump on a hopping sensor is not. To confirm, correlate the detail's suspicious-BAR count and jump sizes with the symptom (a client that stopped working) and the `ctrl_blockack` volume on its channel. First response: note the transmitter, receiver and TID; check whether the triple's own channel was retrying hard at the time (the detail's retry flag is sensor-wide - any channel over the threshold sets it - so read the channel's `retry_pct` directly); the source names no network-side fix (PBAC would defend it but is rarely supported), so locate the transmitter physically (docs/wiki/alert-remediation.md).

**Since #183.** The station whose Block-Ack window is being paralysed (`b.sta`, the BAR's claimed transmitter) can now be the sensor's own radio (`ownership_is_self_mac()`, the #183 item 1 self roster). When it is, the rule still fires, demoted one step (WARN→LOW, or CRIT→WARN when also `mine`) and tagged "self (the sensor's own radio)" in the detail. Note the asymmetry this carries: `self` here names the party the spoofed BAR targets, not an attacker on the sensor's own network, so the tag reads "this was aimed at the sensor" rather than "the sensor did this" — still worth a lower-urgency flag rather than silence, per the issue's own reasoning for the other three rules it touched. The reference-host noise figures above predate this and are not re-measured. Tests: `test_blockack_attack_fires_warn_without_ownership` (baseline, not previously covered through `alerts_update()`), `test_blockack_attack_self_device_demoted_and_tagged` (tests/test_alerts.c). **Corrected 2026-11-11 (#183 item 2 follow-up):** the check is `ownership_is_self_mac()` - the sensor's own interface addresses only - not `ownership_is_own_device()`, which also matched the operator's `--known-mac` roster. As first shipped, a rostered device was tagged "the sensor's own radio", which it is not, and demoted. The station named is the target of the spoofed BAR, so a rostered device being attacked was reported lower than a stranger's. A rostered station now keeps full severity and no tag. Test: `test_blockack_attack_rostered_device_is_not_self`.
