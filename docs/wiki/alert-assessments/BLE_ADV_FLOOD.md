## What the rule checks

Inputs: `s->ble_flood[]` (MAX_BLE_FLOOD 32 rows, include/sloth.h:2798), filled by `src/ble_flood.c` from **every decoded advertisement** on any BLE sensor (`--bt-monitor` HCI reports, `--bt-capture` Ubertooth stream). Each row is one key: (sensor kind, sensor iface, HCI Event_Type, payload family, id, subtype) where the family is Manufacturer Specific Data (id = company ID, subtype = first octet after it) or Service Data 16-bit UUID (id = UUID, no subtype); an advertisement with neither is not counted (src/ble_flood.h:9-19). Per key, **tumbling** windows of `ble.flood_window_s` on the monotonic clock hold: `total` advertisements, `distinct` (address, type) pairs up to BLE_FLOOD_ADDR_CAP = 128, `fresh` = addresses not seen on this key in this window or the two before (three Bloom sets of 4096 bits each, src/ble_flood.c:67-85, 265-277), `baseline` = whether the previous window held anything, and the median of the first 64 RSSI samples (BLE_FLOOD_RSSI_CAP, include/sloth.h:2800; src/ble_flood.h:21-29).

Gates, evaluated in `win_passes()` (src/ble_detect.c:54-64) on the current window, else the previous one (src/ble_detect.c:73-75):

1. `w->baseline && w->fresh >= ble.flood_distinct_addrs` (default 20);
2. `w->total >= ble.flood_rate * window_ms / 1000` (default 10/s over the whole 10 s window = 100 advertisements; the rate is never extrapolated upward from a partial window);
3. `r->spam_known` - the (family, id, subtype) is on the embedded spam list `k_spam[]` (src/ble_flood.c:29-42): Apple 0x004C Continuity 0x07 (Proximity Pair) and 0x0F (Nearby Action), Microsoft 0x0006 beacon 0x03 (Swift Pair), Samsung 0x0075 0x42 (buds) and 0x01 (watch), Google Fast Pair Service Data 0xFE2C. Not tunable.

Key: `bleflood` + sensor iface + adv_type + family name + id + subtype|`none` (src/ble_detect.c:104-112). Detail (src/alerts/rules_ble.c:78-98): `<ADV_TYPE> <mfr|svc_data> 0x<id>/<subtype>: <fresh> new of [>=]<distinct> addresses, <total> adv in <W>s (<rate>/s)[, median <rssi> dBm] via <iface>[ (one-channel sample: a lower bound)] - a documented BLE-spam payload key at flood volume; genuine devices send this key too, so the count and rate are the evidence, not the payload`. `match_ip` NULL - no pcap. WARN, fixed; the CRIT the issue sketched is deferred (src/ble_detect.h:13-24). The finding is re-asserted while the current or previous window passes, so it stops two windows after the flood ends and the engine resolves it 300 s later.

The same `ble_findings_build()` list feeds the `ble_alert` JSONL record, so alert and record cannot disagree (src/ble_detect.h:8-11).

## The attack

Bluetooth LE devices announce themselves with advertising packets; phones and laptops listen for specific vendor payloads to pop up a pairing or "device nearby" card - Apple's Continuity (AirPods proximity pairing, the "Nearby Action" modal), Microsoft's Swift Pair, Google's Fast Pair, Samsung's EasySetup [1][5]. The spam attack forges those payloads at a high rate from a cheap radio so that every handset in range is buried in pop-ups. BleepingComputer (2023-10-25) reported the Flipper Zero "BLE Spam" app offering "eight flood attack options, including: Every method combined, iOS 17 Lockup Crash, Apple Action Modal, Apple Device popup, Android device pair, Windows Device Found", and a claim that "a constant feed of connection notifications renders the device unusable" on a Samsung Galaxy [3]. Bitdefender (2023-10-27) described six platform-tailored floods and named the mitigations: turn off "Show notification" in Nearby Share on Android and "Show notifications to connect using Swift Pair" on Windows [4]. The repo's ATT&CK mapping is T1499 (Endpoint Denial of Service): the cost is the receiving device's pairing UI (include/sloth_alerts.def:302) [1].

What the tool actually puts on the air is the primary source for the rule, read from the Momentum-Apps `ble_spam/` tree [5][6]: `continuity.c` writes AD type 0xFF, company 0x4C and the Continuity type byte (0x07 Proximity Pair, 0x0F Nearby Action); `swiftpair.c` writes company 0x06, beacon ID 0x03; `fastpair.c` lists Service UUID 0xFE2C and Service Data under it; `easysetup.c` writes company 0x75 then 0x42 or 0x01. In `ble_spam.c`, `randomize_mac()` is `furi_hal_random_fill_buf(state->config.address, ...)`, and `start_extra_beacon()` sets the advertising interval to one of `delays[] = {20, 50, 100, 200, 500}` ms and calls `randomize_mac()` **before every payload when the attack's `random_mac` is set** - true for The Kitchen Sink, BT Settings Flood, Android Device Connect, Samsung Buds Popup, Samsung Watch Pair, Windows Device Found and the two Lovense-toy attacks (Vibrate 'em All, Denial of Pleasure); false for the three Apple attacks (iOS 17 Lockup Crash, Apple Action Modal, Apple Device Popup), which use one address per run [6]. That per-payload address is why gate 1 counts **fresh** addresses: the tool's addresses do not survive into the next window, a crowd of genuine devices' addresses do [1][5]. The consequence the repo states plainly: a single-address Apple flood is not this rule's shape and is not detected [1][5].

Apple's iOS 17.2 security notes (2023-12-11) list two Bluetooth fixes - CVE-2023-45866 (keystroke injection) and CVE-2023-42941 (a denial of service "using crafted Bluetooth packets") - without naming the Flipper or BLE Spam; the tool's `continuity.c` labels its crash option "CRASH FIXED IN IOS 17.2" (a UI label, not a code comment), and the repo does not claim the two are the same [5][7][10].

## Evidence that proves it

What sloth records:

- the alert detail: Event_Type, family, id/subtype, `fresh new of distinct addresses`, `total adv in Ws (rate/s)`, median RSSI, the sensor iface, and the one-channel-sample caveat for Ubertooth (src/alerts/rules_ble.c:78-98);
- the `ble_alert` JSONL record, `kind: adv_flood`, with `adv_type`, `family`, `id`, `subtype`, `window` (current/previous), `window_s`, `distinct_addrs` + `distinct_capped`, `fresh_addrs`, `total_adv`, `rate`, `has_rssi` + `median_rssi`, `spam_source` (the `k_spam[]` source line) - change-only, signed on the last **closed** window's fresh/total quantised by powers of two and RSSI by 10 dB, so a steady flood is two records plus heartbeats (docs/wiki/jsonl-schema.md:732);
- `ble_device` records for the advertisers (one per (sensor, address, type)), which during a flood are many short-lived random addresses with the same payload shape;
- sensor_health: `evict_ble_flood` (the 32-key flood table is being churned - a spray of keys, itself suspicious), `evict_ble_device` (the 256-row device table is churning, expected during a flood), `btcap_sampling` (Ubertooth one-channel sample), `ble_adv_reports` / `ble_ext_adv_reports` (docs/wiki/jsonl-schema.md:801,831,834,867);
- **no pcap**: no `match_ip`, and nothing from the HCI channel is written verbatim (src/ble_hci.h:59-63).

To call it real, the reader should see: a listed key with `fresh_addrs >= 20` and `total_adv >= 100` in a 10 s window, with `distinct_addrs` close to `fresh_addrs` (almost every address is new) and a `rate` consistent with one of the tool's 20-500 ms intervals [5][6]; `ble_device` rows for those addresses showing random address types (sub-type bits per Core Spec Vol 6 Part B §1.3.2 [8]) each heard for well under a window; a median RSSI that is roughly constant across the addresses (one transmitter, one place) - a genuine crowd spreads in RSSI; and, if available, people in the room reporting pop-ups at the time (docs/wiki/alert-remediation.md:1759). The strongest form is a flood on several listed keys at once with the same RSSI: that is The Kitchen Sink [6].

What refutes it: `fresh_addrs` well below `distinct_addrs` - many addresses present but few new, i.e. stable advertisers re-announcing (the exact case that made the rule count fresh rather than present [1][5]); a flood only on an Apple key from **one** address (not this rule's shape; a genuine AirPods case in pairing mode advertises Proximity Pair from one address and would not pass gate 1 anyway); `ble_device` rows surviving across windows; a controller with `Filter_Duplicates` on, which reports each address once and makes any observed rate a lower bound, not an inflation [5].

## Benign causes

- **A crowd of genuine devices on one key.** 25 Fast Pair headsets re-advertising every 250 ms are 25 addresses at 100/s on 0xFE2C - gates 2 and 3 pass; gate 1 is designed to fail, because their addresses persist across windows (src/ble_detect.h:40-53) [1][5]. The residual risk is a venue where 20+ *new* Fast Pair / Continuity devices arrive within 10 s - a transit platform, a concert gate - and no source measures that rate, which is why the defaults are a judgement [5].
- **Resolvable-private-address rotation across many devices at once.** Addresses rotate on a recommended 15-minute timer, not synchronised, so a room does not produce 20 fresh addresses in one 10 s window from rotation alone [8].
- **A retail display of many new devices powered on together** (store opening, a shipment of earbuds in pairing mode) could approach gate 1 briefly; the RSSI spread and the persistence of the addresses in later windows distinguish it.
- **An Ubertooth one-channel sample** hears one of the three advertising channels, so it sees roughly a third of the traffic (an under-count of up to two thirds); it is biased toward *missing* floods, not inventing them (`sampled` flag in the detail) [1].

## Assessment

**Severity.** WARN is right. The impact is nuisance-to-DoS of a pairing UI and, on unpatched iOS before 17.2, a lock-up [3][5][7]; nothing is compromised, and the thresholds have no cited basis, so the rule cannot support more [1][5]. The deferred CRIT (src/ble_detect.h:13-24) is correctly deferred.

**Prevalence.** The tooling is consumer-grade, public and widely covered since October 2023 [3][4]; the same payloads run from an ESP32 or nRF52 [5]. No measurement of how often floods actually occur in the wild was found; treat prevalence as "cheap, known, occasional".

**Noise on the reference host.** Not seen (host null) - consistent with either no BLE sensor or no flood. Expected behaviour when it fires: one incident per key; `count` climbs at 4/s; `observations` is inflated: while the current window passes, its live fresh/total/rate change with almost every tick, so the counter climbs at up to the poll rate for part of every window. The `ble_alert` record already signs on the last closed window with power-of-two quantisation (docs/wiki/jsonl-schema.md:732: "never the window in progress (which climbs from zero every window)"); the alert detail should do the same - render the closed window, or quantise - per the AGENTS.md change-only rule. If an operator sees it repeatedly in a venue, raise `ble.flood_distinct_addrs` (2..128) before `ble.flood_rate`; the fresh-address count is the gate that encodes the tool's behaviour [1][5].

**Sufficient to act on?** For a response in the room, yes: the median RSSI and the sensor iface localise the transmitter roughly; the finding names a payload family, never a device (the same code runs on a Flipper, an ESP32 or a laptop) [5].

**First response.** Note the key and the fresh count; confirm with people nearby whether pop-ups appeared; look for the transmitter by RSSI; on handsets apply the vendor mitigations (Nearby Share / Fast Pair notification off on Android, Swift Pair notification off on Windows, iOS >= 17.2) (docs/wiki/alert-remediation.md:1759) [3][4][7].
