## What the rule checks

*This section describes the rule as assessed, before #173. What #173 changed (the detail, and how often the rule fires) is in the **Since #173** paragraph at the end of Assessment; file:line references below are to the assessed tree except `rule_file`.*

**Input.** The `pm_forge` table in `src/pm_forge.c`, fed on the capture thread by `pm_forge_observe_frame()` from every decoded 802.11 **data** frame (type 2) heard on the monitor radio, plus `beacon_find_interval_ms(bssid)` from the beacon table.

**Per-frame logic (pm_forge.c:42-110).** Only station→AP frames count (`to_ds && !from_ds`; A1 = BSSID, A2 = STA). `pm` = Power Management bit; `no_payload` = Null/QoS-Null family (`subtype & 0x04`); `tid` = QoS TID, 0 for non-QoS. Rows are keyed by **station MAC alone**, not (BSSID, STA) - the Power Management bit is a per-station declaration and the inconsistency is visible in the clear header whichever AP eventually acts on it (src/pm_forge.h). Then:
- A Null/QoS-Null with PM=1 records `last_pm1_null_us` (the frame's own capture timestamp, microseconds), its TID, and its evidence-ring event ID (pm_forge.c:74-79).
- A **data frame with payload and PM=0** is a *contradiction* iff a PM=1 Null was recorded, `tid != last_pm1_null_tid`, `now_us >= last_pm1_null_us`, the AP's beacon interval is known (`bms > 0`), and `now_us - last_pm1_null_us <= bms * 1000` (pm_forge.c:80-105). Then `contradiction_count++`, `last_contradiction_ts = now`, and both frames' event IDs enter an 8-slot citation ring (a Null shared across contradictions moves to newest rather than duplicating).
- Same-TID pairs are excluded as "an ordinary immediate wake for more of the same traffic" (docs/views/alerts.md:153). A BSSID never heard beaconing never fires (no window to test against) [1].
- **What does not reset the sleep record.** `last_pm1_null_us` is overwritten only by a newer PM=1 Null. A PM=0 Null, which is the normal way a station announces it is awake, matches neither branch (`no_payload && !pm`, pm_forge.c:74-80) and is ignored. Same-TID PM=0 data does not clear it either (pm_forge.c:106-107 records only `last_pm0_data_*`). So after one sleep declaration, a station that wakes properly and then sends a burst on a second access category within ~100 ms adds one contradiction per frame in that burst.

**Rule (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1098-1127).** Snapshots the table; for every row with `contradiction_count != 0`, fires. `mine = ownership_is_my_bssid(bssid)`.

**Key.** `"pmforge:<sta>"` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1111) - per station, not per (STA, BSSID).

**Detail.** `"<sta> at <bssid>: PM=0 data contradicted a PM=1 sleep declaration <count> time(s) - pattern consistent with Framing Frames queue manipulation, CVE-2022-47522[ - YOUR network]"` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1112-1117). Note the wording discipline (#90): "pattern consistent with", never "attacker is".

**Severity.** WARN; **CRIT when `mine`** (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1124). No confidence value.

**Evidence.** `fire_ev()` with up to `PM_FORGE_EVIDENCE_IDS` = 8 event IDs (= `ALERT_EVIDENCE_IDS`, static-asserted at rules_wifi_mgmt.c:32): the newest contradicting pairs, oldest first [11]. `match_ip = NULL`.

**Escalation / resolution.** `mine` is re-evaluated each tick, so adding `--my-bssid` later escalates; nothing de-escalates. The detail's count moves with each new contradiction → `alert.update` at most once per 60 s. **No recency bound**: unlike `rule_queue_evict` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1166-1191; the bound at 1189-1191 skips rows whose `last_release_ts` is older than `queue_evict.report_secs`, default `QUEUE_EVICT_REPORT_SECS` = 900 s, src/queue_evict.h:74, and the comment at 1173-1188 documents why: saturating the 128-slot engine), this rule re-fires a station for as long as its row survives the 128-entry LRU.

**Engine pressure.** `MAX_PM_FORGE_ENTRIES` (128) equals `MAX_ALERTS` (128, include/sloth.h:823). This is the same pairing that rule_queue_evict's comment (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1173-1182) measured filling the engine and evicting every other rule's findings, and that rule added its recency bound to prevent it. PM_FORGE has no such bound, so a site with more than ~100 stations tripping it once would reproduce that failure. The reference host's 23 keys are well short of it.

**Sensor health.** `evict_pm_forge` (`SH_EVICT_PM_FORGE`, sensor_health.h:65) counts dropped rows.

## The background

The rule is one half of the published "Framing Frames" result (Schepers, Ranganathan & Vanhoef, USENIX Security 2023; CVE-2022-47522) [5][6]. The research observes that a Wi-Fi station signals power-save state with the Power Management bit in the frame-control field, that this field is not protected by encryption even on a secured network, and that the bit is not authenticated, which the paper calls "a fundamental design flaw" [6]. The CVE record states the root cause as the standard not requiring an AP "to purge its transmit queue before removing a client's pairwise encryption key" [5]. The paper's abstract states the researchers "trick access points into leaking frames in plaintext, or encrypted using the group or an all-zero key," and identifies "the unprotected nature of the power-save bit" as the root cause [6]. The affected stacks named are Linux, FreeBSD, iOS and Android; FreeBSD, Cisco and SonicWall issued advisories [5][6][8][9].

What sloth observes is narrower and purely passive: the same station address appearing in two frames whose Power Management bits disagree, close enough in time (inside the AP's own beacon interval) that one honest half-duplex radio should not have produced both. sloth has no key and no location fix, so it reports the *inconsistency as a pattern* and never names a side as the attacker [1][5]. The MITRE mapping is T1557 Adversary-in-the-Middle (Credential Access / Collection) - the queue-manipulation primitive supports an AiTM position [6][12]. The sibling `QUEUE_EVICT` rule (#107 shape B) reports the other observable, the moment buffered traffic is released across a session change, and the two corroborate each other within a 10 s window [1][4].

Scope limits the research itself records: from Linux 5.6 onward the kernel drops data frames with no key available, which prevents the plaintext-after-key-removal method against Linux APs (§3.5.1); the group-key and all-zero-key leaks on other stacks are separate [6]; Cisco calls it "an opportunistic attack" of "minimal value in a securely configured network" [8]. Public proof-of-concept covers the FreeBSD queue-leak and SA-Query timeout variants [7]; the MacStealer tool tests the separate client-isolation bypass, not the power-save inconsistency this rule detects [10].

## Evidence that proves it

**What sloth records.** `alert` / `alert.create` with `title=PM_FORGE`, `sev` WARN or CRIT, `technique=T1557`, `key=pmforge:<sta>`, the detail above, and `evidence_count` / `evidence_id_0..7` citing the contradicting frame pairs (jsonl-schema.md:307-308); `count` = evaluations (ticks), not contradictions (jsonl-schema.md:297, 390). The paired evidence frames are the PM=1 Null and the PM=0 data frame that broke it (pcap-export.md:148) [11].

**pcap.** With `--collect-handshakes` and `--eapol-dir`, the cited frames are written as `<incident>-<event>-PM_FORGE.pcap` under `pcap.min_severity` (default CRIT) and `pcap.key_cooldown_secs` (cli-reference.md:56) - the only route back to the bytes, because a data frame has no IP flow to export on and `match_ip` is NULL [1][11].

**What makes it real (an attack, not a fast wake).** The two cited frames differing in ways one radio cannot fake: a transmitter claiming the victim's address with a **sequence number outside the victim's own sequence space**, or a markedly **different RSSI / radiotap signal** than the victim's other frames (a second transmitter in a different spot) - sloth does not currently compare these, so this must be read from the evidence pcap by hand. A `QUEUE_EVICT` alert for the same station within 10 s (the A+B corroboration the issue defines) is the strongest in-tool signal [1][4]. A rising "N time(s)" with a QUEUE_EVICT companion is the attack's own A-then-B sequence.

**What refutes it (a benign wake).** The two frames sharing the victim's sequence progression and RSSI, a single contradiction that never recurs, no `QUEUE_EVICT` within 10 s, and the station being an ordinary client (phone/laptop) on the operator's own AP doing dynamic power-save - the dominant benign case below.

## Benign causes

- **Ordinary dynamic power-save.** A client announces sleep (PM=1 Null) when its uplink queue drains, then is woken tens to ~100 ms later by new traffic and transmits PM=0 data on a different access category (TID). That is the exact pair the rule flags, and it happens constantly on real phones and laptops. The different-TID and within-beacon-interval guards narrow it but do not remove it: a client genuinely has traffic on a new TID after waking. This is why the CRIT-on-ownership path is dangerous - the operator's own clients are precisely the ones doing this.
- **Capture reordering across channel hops.** sloth hops channels, so it may hear the two frames out of the order they were sent; the rule guards `now_us >= last_pm1_null_us` but timestamp skew near the boundary can still mis-order. The research corpus notes sloth may hear the pair out of order [4].
- **A re-used / randomised station address.** Two devices briefly sharing an address (randomisation collision) look like one station in two power states. Rare, but the per-station-MAC key cannot tell them apart.
- **No benign cause when corroborated.** A PM_FORGE plus a QUEUE_EVICT for the same station in 10 s is the research's own attack signature and has no routine explanation [1][6].

## Assessment

**Severity.** WARN for the bare inconsistency is honest - sloth cannot attribute the forgery and says so. **The ownership→CRIT escalation is too aggressive for this rule.** The operator's own AP is where benign dynamic power-save is most common, and the reference host's CRIT (max_sev=2) on ordinary clients demonstrates the cost: the most serious tier is being spent on routine behaviour, exactly the "bury the type where the operator cares most" failure the `QUEUE_EVICT` design explicitly refuses (it does *not* escalate on ownership alone, rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1156-1163). PM_FORGE should adopt the same discipline: CRIT only with QUEUE_EVICT corroboration, the issue's own A+B rule.

**Prevalence.** The flaw is real and standardised, but the practical window has narrowed (Linux 5.6+ drops keyless frames [6]; "minimal value in a securely configured network" [8]), and no in-the-wild campaign was found in this pass. Treat an uncorroborated PM_FORGE as far more likely to be power-save than attack.

**Reference host - the arithmetic.** 141,288,757 fires on 23 keys = ~6.14 M evaluations per key. Because count is ticks, not contradictions, that is at most ~17.8 days of continuous per-tick re-firing per station (an upper bound: 250 ms is the longest wait between ticks, and the loop wakes early) - i.e. 23 stations that each tripped the inconsistency once or a few times and were then re-reported every tick while their rows stayed resident, with no recency bound to stop them. How long the run was does not follow from this. The CRIT tells us at least one is on a `--my-bssid` AP, the benign-wake case. The noise is structural. Recommendations, in order of value: (1) add a recency bound on `last_contradiction_ts` (a `pmforge.report_secs` knob mirroring `queue_evict.report_secs`) so a station stops re-firing once the inconsistency is stale; (2) require a minimum contradiction count or rate before firing at all; (3) gate CRIT on QUEUE_EVICT corroboration, not ownership; (4) compare sequence number and RSSI of the cited pair so a benign wake (same sequence space) is distinguished from a spoof (foreign sequence) in the rule itself. Only (1) reduces the count: it stops a stale row re-firing. (2) does nothing if the 23 stations already exceed the minimum, and (3) changes only the severity passed to `fire_ev` (rules_wifi_mgmt.c:1124), so it is a severity fix and removes no evaluations.

**Is the evidence sufficient to act on?** The citation of the actual frame pair is the rule's strength - with `--collect-handshakes` a responder can pull the two frames and check sequence/RSSI by hand, which is the decisive device-vs-spoof test. Without that pcap, the alert alone cannot distinguish attack from power-save, and the count tells you nothing (it is ticks). So: act on a PM_FORGE *with* its evidence pcap and/or a QUEUE_EVICT companion; treat a bare, uncorroborated, un-captured PM_FORGE as unproven.

**Responder's first steps.** (1) Check for a `QUEUE_EVICT` on the same station within ten seconds - that pairing is the attack's own sequence and the remediation page says so [4][1]. (2) Pull the evidence pcap (needs `--collect-handshakes`, `--eapol-dir`, `pcap.min_severity<=2`) and compare the two cited frames' sequence numbers and RSSI; a foreign sequence or a different signal says two transmitters. (3) If corroborated and on your network: update AP firmware where the vendor has a CVE-2022-47522 advisory (FreeBSD, Cisco and SonicWall verified via [5][8][9]; D-Link, HPE Aruba and Lenovo are named only by the repo's corpus [1] and were not re-verified in this pass), noting the root cause is in the standard so an unpatched AP stays exposed. (4) sloth names no side as the attacker and neither should the response until the evidence does [1].

**Since #173.** Recommendation (1) is in: a row whose newest contradiction arrived more than `PM_FORGE_REPORT_SECS` (900 s) ago is no longer reported (aged on arrival, `last_contradiction_arrival`, not on the capture stamp: against the capture stamp a replayed savefile older than the bound reported nothing), the incident resolves 300 s later, and a new contradiction opens a new one, so `count` is bounded per incident and a long-lived incident means the contradictions kept coming. The constant is not yet a `--tune` knob. The ownership->CRIT question and (2)-(4) are untouched. Test: `tests/test_alert_tick_rate.c`.

**Since #183.** The contradicting station can now be the sensor's own radio (`ownership_is_own_device()`, #183 item 1). When it is, the rule still fires — a compromised sensor is still worth knowing about — but the severity drops one step (WARN→LOW, or CRIT→WARN when also `mine`) and the detail is tagged "self (the sensor's own radio)" rather than reading as a third-party attacker. This does not change the noise figures above, which predate it and are not re-measured here; it changes only what the operator reads when the station named happens to be the sensor's own card. Tests: `test_pm_forge_self_device_demoted_and_tagged`, `test_pm_forge_self_device_on_my_bssid_is_warn_not_crit` (tests/test_alerts.c).
