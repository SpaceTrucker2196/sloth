Hybrid
everything sloth captured, as before the switch: the default.
Monitor
only what the passive monitor-mode radio heard over the air (beacons, probes, associations, EAPOL, deauths, the channel mix, the 802.11 detectors) and the BLE receivers (--bt-monitor, --bt-capture), which are passive radio too. Nothing the connected Wi-Fi or Ethernet carried: no IP, ARP, DHCP, DNS, mDNS, NBNS, flows or top hosts. Monitor rows keep the monitor radio's dark-grey ground.
Local
only what the host's own connected interfaces carried: Ethernet and the associated Wi-Fi card in managed mode (the IP capture, ARP, DHCP, DNS, mDNS, NBNS, flows, /proc, the interfaces' throughput), and that card's own station dump and scans (Wi-Fi through the local network, not passive). Never the monitor radio.
  • A device seen both ways appears in both scopes, and in each shows only the fields that scope captured: under Monitor no address, hostname or DHCP, mDNS or NBNS name; under Local no SSID, AP bit or probe signal. Its signal, first and last seen and risk are the scope's own (sloth's risk weights over the scope's signals).
  • An alert is in the scope of its rule's input, whatever file the rule is in (CAPTIVE_PORTAL reads DNS, HTTP and TLS: Local). sloth's own-state alerts (NO_MONITOR_MODE, BT_OWN_TRANSMIT) appear in every scope.
  • While sloth says its IP capture is itself a monitor radio (a radiotap link type, or the capture interface is the monitor interface), the stream's IP records count as Monitor's; a banner says so. sloth.db's rows carry no capture path, so its IP tables stay Local.
  • An anomaly is in the scope of what it scored: A2 Monitor, A5-A9 Local, A1 by its metric and key, A3 and A4 (scored on every source's presence) where the device has presence of the scope's own; A10 every scope.
  • The SQL box is unscoped: it runs the raw sloth.db statement it is given, whatever the switch says.
