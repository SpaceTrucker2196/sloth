# IEEE 802.11-2020 §12.7 — the EAPOL-Key messages the harvest rules count

The protocol basis for `PMKID_HARVEST` and `HANDSHAKE_HARVEST` (#172).
**The standard's text was not re-read for this change** (it is not
openly available); the clause numbers and field facts below are the ones
sloth's EAPOL collector already cites and is tested against
(`src/eapol_log.c`, `include/eapol_log.h`, `tests/test_eapol_log.c`),
which is where the harvest tracker gets its classified messages. They
are restated here so a reader of the two alerts can find them, not
newly asserted.

## What the collector relies on, as the tree states it

- **EAPOL-Key frames (§12.7.2).** The Key Replay Counter is an 8-octet
  big-endian field; an M3 carries M1's counter plus one; a candidate
  M1/M2 pair is one where the M2 echoes the M1's counter
  (`eapol_log.c`, the msg == 2 handler). `HANDSHAKE_HARVEST` counts
  only such a pair, once per attempt, so a retransmitted M2 or an M2
  from a different exchange is not a "fresh" handshake.
- **The 4-way handshake (§12.7.6).** M1 and M3 are sent by the
  authenticator, M2 and M4 by the supplicant; the collector refuses a
  message that travelled in the wrong direction. The authenticator
  retries M1 a bounded number of times at bounded intervals and then
  gives up (§12.7.6.6, quoted in `include/eapol_log.h` with the
  defaults it states), which is why `EAPOL_PAIR_WINDOW_S` (10 s) is the
  point after which an M1 with no M2 counts as unanswered.
- **The PMKID KDE.** M1's Key Data may carry a KDE with the selector
  00-0F-AC and data type 4, holding a 16-octet PMKID; the collector
  extracts it as `has_pmkid` (`parse_eapol_key()`), and the export
  writes it as a hashcat `WPA*01` line. Its value, per the 2018
  disclosure, is `HMAC-SHA1-128(PMK, "PMK Name" | MAC_AP | MAC_STA)`
  (`research/papers/steube-2018-pmkid.md`; the issue cites the
  derivation as §12.7.1.3, not checked here).

## Why legitimate cases do not look like harvesting

- **Roaming across a mesh or ESS.** Every association that completes
  runs the handshake: M1, then the station's M2. A station that answers
  its M1s is never "unanswered", however many BSSIDs it visits.
- **PMK caching.** A station resuming a cached PMKSA names a PMKID in
  its (re)association request and the AP may echo one in M1; the
  station, holding the PMK, answers with M2. Same as above.
- **Fast BSS transition.** FT roaming derives keys in the
  authentication/reassociation exchange and does not run the 4-way
  handshake on the new BSS, so it produces no M1 to leave unanswered.
  (Stated from the tree's FT handling elsewhere; the harvest tracker
  simply sees no M1.)

## What it does not support

No clause sets a count of BSSIDs, a ratio of unanswered M1s, a deauth
count or a reconnect delay that separates an attacker from a station.
Those are sloth's judgements, in `src/harvest_track.h`.
