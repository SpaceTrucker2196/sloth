# IEEE 802.11-2020 §11.8 — spectrum management, and what absence means

Clause-annotated summary. The standard text is copyrighted; this is our
own description of the mechanism sloth reads, not an excerpt.

## The three elements that together publish a power ceiling

802.11h folded transmit power control and dynamic frequency selection
into 802.11 so the 5 GHz band could be shared with radar. The power half
is published by an AP in its Beacon and Probe Response frames, across
three elements that each answer a different question:

| Element | Tag | Clause | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Country          | 7  | §9.4.2.8  | what the *regulatory* maximum is here |
| Power Constraint | 32 | §9.4.2.13 | how far below that the *local* maximum sits |
| TPC Report       | 35 | §9.4.2.16 | what the AP is *actually* transmitting at |

The arithmetic §11.8 defines is regulatory maximum (from the Country
element's triplets) minus the local constraint (tag 32) = the local
maximum a station in this BSS may use. A station reads the pair and caps
itself. The TPC Report is the other direction: the AP stating its own
current transmit power and link margin, which is what makes a
closed-loop power decision possible at the station.

Tag 32 is unsigned dB of *reduction* — it has no negative form, which is
why `include/reg_ie.h` stores it `uint8_t` rather than the `int8_t` the
sloth issue's data-model sketch proposed. Both TPC Report fields are
signed: sub-1 mW caps are real, and Link Margin is a difference.

## Why an AP that sends neither is saying nothing

In a regulatory domain where spectrum management applies, the AP sets
the Spectrum Management bit in Capability Information (§9.4.1.4) and
includes these elements. An AP in the 5 GHz plan that advertises neither
tag 32 nor tag 35 has published no ceiling at all: there is no local
maximum for a station to read and no declared transmit power to compare
anything against.

That matters to a passive monitor for a prosaic reason rather than a
cryptographic one. The common cause is a build with the feature compiled
out or switched off — hostapd's `ieee80211d=0` / `ieee80211h=0`, and the
stripped OpenWrt images rogue kits are assembled from, emit no Country
element, no Power Constraint and no TPC Report, while leaving the
default 20 dBm transmit power in place wherever the device is carried.
The absence is therefore a *configuration* signal, not an attack
signature, and sloth reports it as one.

## What absence does not prove

Three limits, stated here because the detector's honesty depends on
them:

1. **A legacy exemption is real.** 802.11a predates 802.11h-2003.
   Pre-802.11h 5 GHz equipment was never required to send either
   element, so its silence is not evidence of anything. sloth suppresses
   the finding when it decoded no HT/VHT/HE/EHT capability element —
   `phy == "legacy"`.

2. **6 GHz is a different mechanism.** The element carrying the power
   limit for a 6 GHz BSS is the Transmit Power Envelope (tag 195), not
   Power Constraint or TPC Report. TPE itself is older — 802.11ac-2013
   defined it for VHT — but 802.11ax-2021 extended it with the PSD and
   regulatory-client interpretations and the Transmit Power Category
   that 6 GHz relies on.
   Reporting the absence of tags 32 and 35 on 6 GHz would name the wrong
   element as missing, so the detector is confined to 5 GHz. 5 GHz and
   6 GHz channel *numbers* overlap, so the band cannot be read from the
   number alone — see `ap_band_label()`.

3. **A receiver that was elsewhere produces the same observation.** A
   channel-hopping radio misses most beacons from most BSSIDs. Two
   things make absence mean something anyway: the retained envelope is
   per element and never cleared, so one beacon carrying the element at
   any point in the session is enough to settle the question
   permanently; and the detector requires the BSS to have been under
   observation for a floor of seconds before it will read silence as a
   finding.

## Related sloth alert kinds

`ALERT_TYPE_TPC_ABSENT_HIGH_BAND` — WARN, and only for a BSS the
operator designated with `--my-bssid` / `--my-ssid`. It is a compliance
finding about the operator's own equipment, with no ATT&CK technique
assigned: nothing was transmitted at sloth to produce it, and the
technique the issue proposed (T1557.004, Evil Twin) would assert
impersonation from a configuration gap whose likeliest cause is a
stripped build.
