# T1200 — Hardware Additions

An adversary introduces physical hardware — an implant, a dropbox, a
rogue access point, a laptop plugged into a spare port — to gain access
that no software control was watching for.

The defence is inventory. You cannot notice an addition without knowing
what was there before, which is the whole reason sloth keeps a persisted
device table rather than a session-scoped one.

## Why sloth cites this

`ALERT_TYPE_UNKNOWN_DEVICE` fires on a MAC address seen on the network
that is not in the operator's roster.

This is the alert most dependent on operator input and the most honest
about it: with no roster supplied, *every* device is unknown and the
rule is useless noise. It becomes valuable exactly in proportion to how
well the roster is maintained, which is a property worth stating rather
than hiding behind a default.

The persisted `devices` table is what makes "not seen before" answerable
across sessions. A first-seen timestamp from a database that started an
hour ago means nothing.

## What it does not say

**MAC randomisation has largely broken the naive form of this.** Modern
phones present a different address per network and rotate it, so an
unrostered MAC is far more often a guest's handset than an implant.
Sloth's seqnum correlation (`[j]`) exists to partially undo this, and it
is partial.

**Presence is not intrusion.** A neighbour's device associating to the
wrong SSID, a contractor, a visitor — these are the ordinary causes, and
they outnumber the interesting one by a wide margin.

**Absence from the roster is not absence of authorisation.** The roster
is a record of what someone remembered to write down.
