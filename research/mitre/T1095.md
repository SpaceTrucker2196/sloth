# T1095 — Non-Application Layer Protocol

Command and control over something below the application layer, where
most inspection is not looking. ICMP is the usual choice: it is almost
always permitted outbound, it carries an arbitrary payload, and very
few networks log it.

## Why sloth cites this

`ALERT_TYPE_ICMP_TUNNEL` watches the **payload**, not the rate.

An echo request's data field exists to be echoed back; its contents are
conventionally a fixed pattern or a timestamp, and every common `ping`
implementation fills it predictably. A tunnel has to put bytes
somewhere, and this is the only field with room — so the signal is
payload size and entropy inconsistent with any `ping` that would
plausibly have produced it.

Rate is deliberately not the trigger. A tunnel carrying an interactive
shell is *slow*: a few packets a second, indistinguishable from a
monitoring ping by volume alone.

## What it does not say

**Large ICMP payloads have legitimate uses.** Path-MTU discovery,
deliberate `ping -s` diagnostics, and some vendor keepalives all produce
unusual sizes. Entropy is the discriminator that survives those, and it
is not perfect.

**An encrypted tunnel and a compressed one look alike.** High entropy
says the bytes are not a `ping` pattern. It does not say what they are.

**Absence proves little.** A tunnel that keeps its payload small and
low-entropy — at the cost of throughput — is not detected here, and a
patient adversary will accept that cost.
