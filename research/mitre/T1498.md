# T1498 — Network Denial of Service

Exhausting the capacity a service depends on. **.001 Direct Network
Flood** is the plain form: send enough of something that nothing else
gets through.

On 802.11 the resource is not bandwidth. It is **airtime**, and it is
shared by every radio in earshot regardless of which network they belong
to. That makes wireless denial of service unusually cheap and unusually
indiscriminate.

## Why sloth cites this

Five alerts, four of which are flood rates and one of which is the
symptom rather than the cause:

**`DEAUTH_FLOOD`** — the classic. Spoofed deauthentication frames
disconnect clients that have no way to tell a real one from a forged
one. Management-frame protection kills it, which is why sloth also
watches the shapes it moved to (see `SA_QUERY_FLOOD` and `BTM_ABUSE`).

**`BEACON_FLOOD`** — hundreds of fabricated networks. Costs the
attacker almost nothing and fills every client's scan list, which is
`mdk4 b`'s entire function.

**`ASSOC_FLOOD`** — association requests at a rate no roaming policy
produces, filling an AP's association table.

**`RTS_FLOOD`** — the quiet one. An RTS asks every radio in range to
stay silent for the duration it names. A flood of them silences a
channel *without transmitting anything a frame-rate detector was
watching for*, which is why sloth checks the Duration field against the
legal maximum rather than only the rate.

**`RF_DEGRADED`** is not a flood detector. It reports retry and
bad-FCS ratios — the observable a flood produces, and also the
observable a microwave oven produces.

## What it does not say

**It does not distinguish attack from congestion**, and neither does
sloth. A saturated channel at a conference and a channel under attack
look similar from the outside; the difference is usually in *who* is
transmitting, which is why the flood rules key on a source where the
frame carries one.

`RF_DEGRADED` is stated as an observable rather than a claim of intent
precisely because it cannot make that distinction at all. It maps to
T1498 because that is where an operator will look for it, not because
sloth believes an adversary is present.

**Control frames name no BSSID.** CTS and ACK carry a receiver address
and nothing else, so a flood of them can be attributed to a channel and
to nothing else. Sloth reports what the frame carries rather than
inventing an attribution — see `src/ctrl_frames.h`.
