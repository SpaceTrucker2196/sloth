# T1557.004 — Adversary-in-the-Middle: Evil Twin

"Adversaries may host seemingly genuine Wi-Fi access points to deceive
users into connecting to malicious networks." The technique page
(version 1.1, created 2024-09-17, last modified 2025-04-15, read
2026-10-26) goes on to the step that makes the impostor pay off: "Upon
logging into the malicious Wi-Fi access point, a user may be directed to
a fake login page or captive portal webpage to capture the victim's
credentials." It records APT28 use of Wi-Fi Pineapple
tooling.

## Which sloth findings map here

- `CAPTIVE_PORTAL`, its `portalkit:` findings (#169): a named evil-twin
  kit's default portal page served on the wire. That is the
  credential-harvest page this technique describes, identified by the kit
  that produced it. Sources for each fingerprint:
  `research/tools/captive-portal-kits.md`.
- `SAE_PSK_SPLIT` (#74): the PSK-only half of a split SSID is the lane an
  evil twin wants a client to take.

`CAPTIVE_PORTAL` as a whole is tagged with the parent, T1557: its probe
findings (a connectivity check answered wrongly, a sentinel resolving
locally) are adversary-in-the-middle whether or not an evil twin is the
middle — a wired MITM or a hostile resolver produces them too. The kit
findings are the part that is specifically this sub-technique. One alert
type carries one technique, so the parent is the honest tag for the type
and this document is where the sub-technique is cited.

## DET0379 — what MITRE's detection strategy actually says

"Detect Evil Twin Wi-Fi Access Points on Network Devices" (DET0379,
version 1.0, created 2025-10-21, last modified 2026-05-12, read
2026-10-26). Its analytic AN1069 watches for multiple APs sharing an SSID
with different BSSIDs or encryption, probe responses from unauthorised
APs, and "failed authentication requests redirected to non-standard
portals". Its tunable elements include a known-SSID baseline, an allowed
BSSID list and a CaptivePortalDomains parameter — "trusted login domains;
unrecognized portals may be malicious".

Stated carefully, because #169 quoted it more strongly than it reads:
DET0379 does **not** name the OS connectivity-check probes, and does not
recommend watching their responses. What it does support is the shape of
#169's grading — a portal is suspicious *relative to a baseline* of the
operator's own networks (`--my-ssid` / `--my-bssid` here, the
KnownSSIDs / AllowedBSSIDs elements there), and a portal on a network
that should have none is the signal. The probe-response mechanism itself
is cited from the vendors in `research/tools/connectivity-check-sentinels.md`.
