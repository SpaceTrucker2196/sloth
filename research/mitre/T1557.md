# T1557 — Adversary-in-the-Middle

An adversary positions themselves between two communicating parties to
support follow-on collection or manipulation. On Wi-Fi this is most
often a rogue access point the victim associates to, whether by being
lured, being steered, or simply finding it stronger than the real one.

## How sloth's rules map to it

Several detectors share this technique because they catch different
*stages* of the same attack:

- `EVIL_TWIN` / `EVIL_TWIN_PROXIMITY` — the rogue AP exists and is
  close.
- `KARMA_AP` — it is answering probes for networks it has never
  advertised, to be found.
- `CSA_ABUSE` — it is steering clients onto a channel it controls.
- `CAPTIVE_PORTAL` — it is intercepting the connectivity check to open
  a browser on the victim.
- `PEAP_NO_SERVER_CERT` — a client accepted it without verification.

## The mapping decision worth knowing

`BTM_ABUSE` deliberately maps to **T1498** (Network Denial of Service)
rather than here. What sloth observes is a client forced off its AP;
whether it lands on an attacker is a separate observation. Tagging the
forcing as AiTM would claim the second half of the attack on the
evidence of the first.
