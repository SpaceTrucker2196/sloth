# CVE-2022-47522 — Framing Frames

**An AP decides whether to buffer a station's frames purely from the
Power Management bit the station sets in its own, unencrypted MAC
header.** IEEE 802.11-2020 §9.2.4.1.7 defines PwrMgt as part of Frame
Control, which is never encrypted — not even on a protected BSS, since
encryption covers the frame body, not the header. The standard does
not require the AP to clear a station's transmit queue, or to verify
the station's identity some other way, before honouring that bit. An
attacker who spoofs a victim's source address can therefore announce
"I am entering power-save" on the victim's behalf with a forged
PM=1 Null or QoS-Null frame, and the AP starts queuing the victim's
downlink traffic for later delivery — delivery whose circumstances
(shape B of this issue: a release following an attacker's own
reassociation) the standard also does not pin down. D-Link, HPE Aruba
Networking, Cisco and Lenovo each issued advisories; the root cause is
in the 802.11 standard itself; not everyone's firmware is patched.

Found by Mathy Vanhoef and Domien Schepers (credited in the NVD entry
as Vanhoef & Bhupathiraju in follow-on writeups), published at USENIX
Security 2023 as "Framing Frames: Bypassing Wi-Fi Encryption by
Manipulating Transmit Queues".

## Why sloth cites this

**`PM_FORGE`** is the directly observable half: two frames claiming one
source address, in two incompatible power-save states, inside the
window the AP itself would still be honouring the sleep declaration
for (its own beacon interval). One honest, half-duplex station cannot
produce that pair. Sloth has no key and no location fix, so it reports
the contradiction as a pattern, never an identity — the same
discipline #90 established for KARMA/PMKID wording.

**`QUEUE_EVICT`** is the other observable half: the moment the buffer
comes out. The queue outlives the security context it was filled
under. 802.11 does not require an AP to discard a station's pending
frames when that station's session changes, so the paper shows two
routes to them — reassociating under the victim's address, which makes
the AP release the frames encrypted under the *new* session's key, and
taking the frames after the victim is disconnected, when the key has
already been removed from the AP's key store and the frames go out
with no encryption at all. Passively, both look the same: a
session-change frame for one (BSSID, STA), then an immediate burst of
unicast downlink frames to that station.

That observable is weak on its own and the detector says so. An AP
that has just admitted a station has traffic for it, so **ordinary
roaming produces the identical shape** — which is why `QUEUE_EVICT`
reports LOW and the word `uncorroborated` by default, and only reaches
WARN when `PM_FORGE` has flagged the same station within ten seconds
of the release. That pairing is the attack's own sequence: the PM bit
is what makes the AP start buffering, and the session change is what
makes it stop.

## What it does not say

**It does not identify which frame is the forgery.** Sloth sees a
station-address claim it with PM=1, then the same claim transmitting
with PM=0 shortly after, on a different access category. Either frame
could in principle be the spoof; sloth reports the contradiction, not
an accusation against one side.

**It is not, by itself, the AP releasing the queued frames, or an
A-MSDU client-isolation bypass (shape C).** `QUEUE_EVICT` above covers
the release; shape C needs decrypted subframe bodies neither rule
reads. `PM_FORGE` covers the PM-bit contradiction only.

**`PM_FORGE` requires a known beacon interval.** Without having heard
the BSSID beacon at least once, sloth has no window to test the gap
against and does not guess one — a BSSID never heard beacon stays
silent rather than firing on an invented constant. `QUEUE_EVICT` has no
such dependency and no such grounding either: 802.11 fixes no deadline
for flushing a buffered frame, so its 100 ms window is a chosen bound
rather than one read off the air.

**`QUEUE_EVICT` does not say an attack happened.** Uncorroborated it is
a LOW observation whose own text names the benign explanations, because
the shape it matches is also what every ordinary roam looks like. It
also cannot distinguish the two release routes from the outside — it
reports which session-change frame opened the window, not whether the
frames that followed were encrypted under a new key or under none.
