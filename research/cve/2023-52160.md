# CVE-2023-52160 — wpa_supplicant PEAP bypass

With a common misconfiguration, `wpa_supplicant` will complete a PEAP
authentication against a server that presented **no valid TLS
certificate at all**. The client associates, EAPOL completes, traffic
flows, and nothing about the session looks wrong from the outside.

Millions of Android and ChromeOS handsets shipped in the affected
configuration. The fix is upstream, but a handset that has not taken the
update remains vulnerable and remains the operator's to configure.

## The passive signal

In a sound TLS-in-EAP exchange the AP sends a **ServerHello** and a
**Certificate** before the client commits, and those records travel
unencrypted inside EAP-Request frames — visible to a monitor-mode radio.
Their *absence* before EAP-Success is the diagnostic.

## What the detector can and cannot claim

sloth does not reassemble EAP-TLS fragments, so the two halves carry
different confidence and the alert says which:

- **no ServerHello** — the AP never began a TLS handshake. Unambiguous.
- **no Certificate observed** — a ServerHello was seen and a Certificate
  was not. Probably certless; possibly a long chain that crossed a
  fragment boundary.

## Remediation

The client needs `ca_cert` set and `domain_suffix_match` configured.
Without a CA pin, "verify the server" has nothing to verify against.
