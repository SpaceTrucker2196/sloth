# CERT/CC VU#723755 — WPS PIN brute force

WPS's "external registrar" authentication method needs nothing but the
AP's 8-digit PIN. The advisory's finding is that the protocol leaks how
much of a guess was right: *"When the PIN authentication fails the
access point will send an EAP-NACK message back to the client. The
EAP-NACK messages are sent in a way that an attacker is able to
determine if the first half of the PIN is correct."* The last digit is
a checksum over the other seven, so the search collapses from 10^8 to
*"10^4 + 10^3 which is 11,000 attempts in total."*

The second half of the advisory is about the mitigation rather than the
flaw: *"It has been reported that many wireless routers do not
implement any kind of lock out policy for brute force attempts. This
greatly reduces the time required to perform a successful brute force
attack."* The impact is full network compromise — *"brute force the WPS
PIN and retrieve the password for the wireless network, change the
configuration of the access point, or cause a denial of service"* — and
the only workaround CERT offers is to turn the external registrar off.

CVSS base 9.3. Date public 2011-12-27. Reported by Stefan Viehböck.

## Why sloth cites this

Two rules, reading the same attack from the two sides it is visible
from.

`ALERT_TYPE_WPS_PIN_BRUTE` counts the advisory's own unit of work. One
PIN guess is one M1→M3→EAP-NACK restart cycle: the NACK *is* the
oracle, so it is also the thing that can be counted off the air without
decrypting anything. The owner-accepted threshold is five cycles in
60 s from a station (decision of 2026-09-30, configurable via
`--wps-pin-brute-cycles`). Sloth deliberately does not count a NACK
that did not follow M3 — that is an ordinary M2D-style refusal, and
folding it in would inflate every figure with normal traffic. The rule
also aggregates by UUID-E across rotating station addresses, because
the enrollee identity inside M1 is not what an attacker rotating its
MAC is trying to hide.

`ALERT_TYPE_WPS_LOCKOUT_CYCLING` reads the advisory's lockout sentence
the other way round. Where a router *does* implement the lock-out
policy CERT says many lack, a brute force shows up as the AP repeatedly
locking and releasing, and that transition is advertised in every
beacon by the AP Setup Locked bit. So the sawtooth is attack evidence
even when every PIN attempt was missed — which on a channel-hopping
receiver is the normal case, since beacons are continuous and the EAP
exchange is not. Threshold: two completed locked→unlocked cycles per
hour, configurable via `--wps-lockout-cycles`.

## What it does not say

The advisory gives no detection signature and no rate: it describes a
design flaw and an attempt count, not what an attack looks like on the
air. The numeric thresholds above are the issue author's proposal
accepted by the repo owner, not CERT's — see `docs/views/alerts.md`.

It also names no specific vendor or chipset. Sloth ships no weak-RNG
vendor watchlist for that reason, and the offline Pixie-Dust variant of
this attack is a separate mechanism with no rule here yet.
