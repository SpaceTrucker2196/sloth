# CERT/CC VU#871675 — Dragonblood

WPA3's SAE handshake and its transition modes carry design and
implementation weaknesses collectively named Dragonblood. The advisory
covers side-channel leaks in the SAE password-derivation, downgrade
paths in WPA3-Transition mode, and denial-of-service against the
handshake itself.

## Why sloth cites this

`ALERT_TYPE_WPA_DOWNGRADE` fires on the *preconditions* this advisory
describes rather than on an exploit attempt. Two of its four kinds come
straight from here:

- **MFP capable-but-not-required on a SAE BSS.** WPA3 mandates
  management-frame protection; optional-not-required is a downgrade
  primitive, because a client that would have insisted is permitted not
  to.
- **WPA2/WPA3 transition mode.** PSK and SAE on offer simultaneously is
  legitimate during a migration window and is also the state an attacker
  needs the AP to be in.

`ALERT_TYPE_SAE_PSK_SPLIT` cites the same advisory for a different
shape. Dragonblood's downgrade paths assume a client can be steered onto
the PSK lane; the split is what that looks like on the air when the two
lanes are offered by *different BSSIDs* under one SSID rather than by
one AP in transition mode. The advisory covers the mechanism; which of
the two rules fires depends only on whether the weak lane is on the same
radio as the strong one.

## What it does not say

The advisory does not give a detection signature — these are
configuration postures, not attack traffic. sloth reports what the AP
advertises about itself and leaves the judgement of whether a migration
window is still justified to the operator.
